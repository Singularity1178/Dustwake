# Dustwake architecture

Content: the tutorial dungeon (with its first NPC, the Treasury Guardian), plus the surface slice (rune travel, harvesting), a catalog-driven build menu with deconstruction, crafting at a bench (planks, cut stone, carved runes), and golem gear slots with an RPG-style paper-doll pack. This structure is the smallest one I could find that lets the planned game (surface, harvesting, construction, NPCs, management screens) grow without another rewrite. Every abstraction here is used either by the tutorial or by the tests.

## Layers

```

               monologue lines, cutscene scripts, the day/night cycle (daycycle.ts),
```

**The main boundary is that the World does not know presentation exists.** `world/` and `sim/` never touch canvas, audio or `Math.random`, and the tests import them straight into Node. Presentation reads the World, calls sim entry points, and drains `world.events` to play sound and spawn particles. `view/audience.ts` classifies each event as the player's, nearby (same area as the player), or elsewhere: only the player's events drive notes, toasts, screens and shakes; nearby actors get local sound and particles; remote actors are silent. This boundary matters most for this genre. NPCs, animals and jobs will run every tick, including off-screen and in tests, and they have to be saveable. None of that is possible while sim code fires `sfx.*` and `setTimeout` inline.

## Key decisions

### Actor foundations (v10)
`content/actors.ts: ACTORS` is the one registry for bodies, art keys, speed, harvest power, inventory tiers, gear slots, power requirements and capabilities. The player, guardian, worker golem and non-golem worker are rows. `worker` is an optional capability, not an actor type. `NPCS` only gives a dialogue identity and its actor definition. Since v12 one worker golem stands dormant in the artificery (see "Production rollout (v12)"). The non-golem worker (`npc_worker`, art key `human`) is a test fixture: no builder, NPCS row or migration produces one, so its plain inline drawing in `view/actors.ts` is a stand-in no production path can show (`tests/rollout.test.ts` checks the spawns).

`Actor.definition` selects its row; `upgrades`, `gear`, `powerCore`, interaction `mode` and durable `job` belong to that actor. Capacity and slot requirements read `actorFlags`, not another actor's body. The existing `hasFlag` / `setFlag` / `clearFlag` API redirects the tutorial's `golem.arm` and `golem.slot.*` names to the player's upgrades for compatibility. They are no longer world flags. The eye repair and story knowledge remain progression flags. All new state is part of save v10, with a v9 migration; the opening and dialogue scripts are unchanged.

### Body power
`content/power.ts: POWER_CORES` maps an item to a family, tier and optional speed/harvest effects. `Actor.powerCore` is a separate socket from `Actor.gear.core` (the attachment mount). The tutorial's aether core is not body fuel. `coreCheck` distinguishes **fits** from **sufficient**: a Ruby Golem Core can be inserted into the guardian, but does not power its higher-rank body. Effects apply only while the requirement is satisfied. Insertion and removal are walk-up sim commands; replacement returns the old core through the ordinary inventory/overflow path. An unpowered worker pauses its job and its current task; supplying power resumes the same work. Speech/wake story flags remain independent of power, so the existing guardian encounter is unchanged.

**Slotting in play (v12): one seam, both ways.** `sim/commands.ts: orderCoreSwap(w, a, target, item | null)` validates and starts `CORE_TASK` (`content/interactions.ts`, a 0.9 s reach) with the `slotCore` effect: `item` puts that core in, `null` takes the core out. When the reach lands, `sim/power.ts: applySlotCore` re-validates through `insertPowerCore` / `removePowerCore` (reach, sight, fit, still carried) and emits `coreSlotted` `{actor, target, item, inserted, powered}`. That is the player's event (`powerChanged` belongs to the body, so the audience filter would treat it as a nearby actor's), and it alone drives the host's feedback: going in, a latch, a rising hum and embers from the hatch, then (a worker golem) the boot-up and stand animation; coming out, the latch, a falling hum (only if the body ran on it), dust and (a worker golem) the power-down; a note either way. The input is its own key, **C** on the play screen (`PlayerInput.core` -> `coreKey`): facing a body that takes cores (any actor whose definition has `power`), it takes the core out if there is one, else slots the first fitting core carried; facing nothing, nothing happens. E never slots anything, so the player can always talk to the guardian while carrying a core, and nothing happens by accident. The management screen calls `orderCoreSwap` with an explicit choice (its first row; `C` inside it does the same). No path creates or deletes a core: insertion takes it from the pack, removal gives it back through `giveItem` (a full pack drops it on the ground), a replacement returns the old one. The game's one Ruby Golem Core moves between the worker, the guardian and the pack any number of times (tested, across a save/load).

**Three states** for a body with a power requirement (`view/actors.ts: actorActive`, `workerArt.ts: workerState`). The sim only knows these three; how the body gets between them is presentation (below).
- *Unpowered*: no core, or one too weak. A worker stops (its job pauses). The worker golem is crouched, head tucked, hands on its knees, lens lid down, the hatch dark. No glow at all.
- *Powered-idle*: a sufficient core, no task and no running order. It does nothing: idle workers never wander (`tickJob` only stops it). After a few seconds on standby it settles into the same crouch, the only visible change from unpowered being the core glowing faintly in its hatch (`WORKER_CORE.idle`, 0.38 of full): dormant, not active.
- *Working*: powered, with a task or a running order. It stands, lamp lit and core bright, walks with a stomping gait and swings into its task.

**The worker golem's look and animation (`workerArt.ts`, `workerAnim.ts`).** The worker is the cheap rank below the maintainer: small (about 18 px against 24), slip-cast red clay banded with blackened iron, a barrel body on stump legs with flat iron-shod feet, big fingerless mitts (it cannot build), one cyclops lens behind a visor slot with an iron lid, a lifting ring on its head, the belt doubling as the buckle of the ruby core's hatch with a stencilled tally mark beside it, and an iron vent plate on the back. `WorkerPose` is continuous (`rise` crouched to standing, `neck`, `yaw`, `arms`, `shake`, `dip`, plus `swing` / `reach` for tasks), so animations are keyframes, not frame swaps. `workerAnim.ts` is a pure sequencer (no DOM, no sim writes, deterministic, tested headlessly in `tests/workerAnim.test.ts`) that the host steps each frame with `{ powered, active, moving, hasCore }` (`game.ts: updateWorkerLook`; a look that was not stepped for half a second starts at rest in whatever the sim says, so a loaded game or a return to the area plays nothing). Its modes:
- *boot* (a core goes in, 3.6 s): the core sparks and gutters, the lamp sputters on, the head comes up and looks left then right, the arms unfold, it rises and plants its feet, then stands on standby.
- *settle* (standing idle for `IDLE_DELAY`, 3.5 s, then 2.2 s): one slow scan and a blink, the lamp dims, arms fold, it lowers itself into the crouch.
- *wake* (dormant and given an order, 0.6 s) and the same quick stand when a booting body has to move.
- *shutdown* (the core comes out, 2.6 s): a jolt, lamp and core gutter out, the head turns aside and drops, the arms go slack, it sags into the crouch. It plays relative to the posture it was in, so a dormant body only gutters.
Every switch blends from the previous frame's channels (no pops, tested over interrupted transitions). Sound and dust come from named cues (`spark`, `creak`, `lamp`, `plant`, `jolt`, `lampOut`, `settle`) the host drains. Task motion is derived from the actor's task (`taskMotion`: a harvest `punch` is an overhead blow landing at its fire point, `reach` and `craft` push the arms out); a body that is down never walks or works. **Rune travel has no body animation on purpose:** a worker channelling a rune is drawn exactly as one standing (tested pixel for pixel), and the host plays the rune's own effect around it: it is lifted off the ground (`Look.lift`), motes spiral into the rune and the light builds, with a chime and a flash where it leaves and where it lands (`game.ts: updateWorkerLook`, `workerTravelled`). The worker's core and lamp also light the floor round it. `tests/support/workerPreview.ts` and `scripts/preview.mjs` render contact sheets of poses and animation strips to `.preview/` (`node scripts/preview.mjs`) for checking the art without a browser.

The guardian given the ruby core stays unpowered (it fits, it isn't sufficient): its story, awake flag and eye light don't change.

### Orders and navigation
`Actor.job` is a durable order above the short task queue, not a queue of work copied in advance. `content/jobs.ts: JOB_KINDS` gives the initial phase and required capabilities; `sim/jobs.ts: JOB_HANDLERS` validates arguments and advances one kind. The built-in proofs are gather/deposit, craft-at-station and walking. Arguments, phase, completion count, stable prop references, retry state, task receipts and walking waypoints are plain saved data. Any capable actor can run them, including the player; only the walk-up management command requires the target's `worker` capability.

- **Execution.** A handler approaches a target, then starts the existing task/effect path. `Task.jobId` identifies its owning order. The effect writes a result at its fire point, so a save after consuming materials but before the animation ends never crafts or deposits twice. Retasking cancels only the previous job's work, not an unrelated manual task. `enterArea` still discards area-bound tasks/queued work, but keeps the job and clears only its local navigation. Workers never open player screens or trigger the player's tutorial completion.
- **Travel.** A route is derived from all rune links, respecting activation, one-way edges and links that awaken their destination. Each hop is walk, channel, travel, then continue the original order. A travel task records its chosen destination, so a branching network does not silently take the first link. Arrival uses the traveller's body, excludes itself from blockers and refuses if no free spot exists; it never falls back to overlapping a body. Missing runes and blocked arrivals become blocked orders.
- **Navigation.** `sim/navigation.ts` caches a static grid on `Area.layoutRevision` and body dimensions. Four-way edges and the off-grid approach are swept at 2 px intervals. Other actors are transient blockers, checked during planning and every movement step, not part of the static cache. Waypoints are saved; grids are derived. A changed layout is rechecked before walking through it. Movement is body-aware, speed-limited and deterministic for the same dt/input sequence; the player's original limp and acceleration stay unchanged.
- **Stuck work.** Actors advance in stable id order. After a short lack of progress, the lower-priority actor tries a reachable passing place outside the higher-priority route. It walks there with collision enabled, then waits before resuming. If no solution exists, bounded replans and retries settle into an explicit `blocked` order with a reason, rather than an endless active-task loop. `resumeJob` or a new order retries it after the obstruction is fixed. Full storage, missing resources/tools/stations and full output packs follow the same policy. Idle workers do not wander.

### Storage and shared crafting
`PropKindDef.storage` declares capacity; `PropInst.inventory` holds slots. Builders may declare initial contents in `PropDef.inventory`; runtime copies them, and an emptied authored chest saves an explicit empty diff so its contents cannot respawn. `sim/inventoryOps.ts` contains owner-independent counting, insertion and removal; `repackInto` also accepts any inventory owner. `sim/storage.ts` resolves actor/prop references to short-lived snapshots and commits a transaction only after validating it. Prop commits always call `Area.setInventory`; no caller mutates a live prop's slot array. Transfers stop at capacity and leave the remainder at the source. Deconstruction checks every stored critical item and spills other contents before refunding the prop.

Craft sources are the actor's pack, then storage within two tiles (Chebyshev, measured from the station anchor), ordered by stable prop id. Ingredient rows are aggregated before checking; consumption revalidates and commits atomically at the effect's fire point. Two crafters competing for one chest cannot partly consume a recipe. Tools must be in the actor's own pack and are never consumed. New UI/orders always bind the station and recheck its kind, area and reach; the stationless path remains for legacy queued craft tasks. Jobs wait if the output will not fit; the player's existing overflow-to-ground behaviour is unchanged.

Inverse recipes are ordinary `CraftRecipe` rows with `mode: "deconstruct"`, production rows since v12: UNMAKE BROKEN CROWN (the crown into the High-Grade Ruby) and UNMAKE RUNE OF SWIFT / UNMAKE RUNE OF GLOW (each into one stone). Their names stay distinct and within the crafting list's 20 characters. They never participate in `rawValue`, nor in what counts as a crafted material. Alternative forward rows have deterministic id ordering and may explicitly opt into/out of valuation; recursion has a cycle guard. Relic credit is now discovery (`relic.found.*`), not possession, so dismantling or dropping the crown cannot reduce the relics display.

E on a storage prop emits `openStorage`; the host opens the storage screen (see "Worker management and storage screens"). Its moves are `sim/commands.ts: moveStorageSlot`, on `sim/storage.ts: transferSlot`.


### Worker management and storage screens
Two overlays behind `UiHost`, registered in `Game.makeScreens` and opened from the host's `onEvent`, only for the player's own event and only from the play screen (so a worker's events, or a player event arriving under another screen, never open or replace anything; `view/audience.ts` already filters the first). The world pauses under them; they hold no saved state (cursor memory aside), so **there is no schema change and no migration**.

- **Storage screen** (`screens/storage.ts`, `StorageUi`). Pack on the left (8 / 16 / 48 slots, the pack's tiers), the chest on the right (`PropKindDef.storage.slots`, 32 for the storage chest), a description panel below. Movement keys move the cursor; `A` off the chest's first column crosses to the pack, `D` off the pack's last column to the chest, on the same row or the nearest the other pane has. `E` moves the whole stack under the cursor, `Q` one item, `Esc` / `I` / `Tab` close. Every move is `sim/commands.ts: moveStorageSlot(w, a, propId, "store" | "take", slot, n)`, which checks the prop is there and in reach, then calls `sim/storage.ts: transferSlot`, a slot-exact sibling of `transferItems` (so the cursor stays on the stack it is moving). As much as fits moves; the result is `ok`, `partial`, `empty`, `full`, `critical`, `gone` or `far`, each with a player message. Items that cannot be dropped (the tutorial's) are never stored, so progress cannot be put out of reach. Nothing is mutated by the screen; the chest's slots are saved with the prop as before.
- **Management screen** (`screens/manage.ts`, `ManageUi`). Left: the worker's portrait (its `BODY_ART` in its current state), power (`sim/orders.ts: powerState`), area, order, phase, status (`WORKING`, `PAUSED`, `WAITING FOR POWER`, `BLOCKED: STORAGE FULL`, `DONE`), its carried slots and worn gear. Right: a row list (core, GATHER, CRAFT, GO TO, pause / resume / retry, cancel) and three small forms. `W`/`S` choose, `A`/`D` change a value, `E` confirms, `C` slots the core, `Esc` goes back and then closes. The forms use the builders `gatherOrder`, `craftOrder` and the new `walkOrder`; kinds the worker lacks the capabilities for (`JOB_KINDS`) are greyed out.
  - *Only valid targets.* `sim/orders.ts` derives every list from the World and data rows, never from stored state: resources from `HARVEST`; source areas that are visited, reachable (`canReach`: the worker is there or `runeRoute` leads there) and still hold something that yields the resource; storage props (`propDef(kind).storage`) and stations (`propDef(kind).station`) in reachable areas; recipes from `CRAFT_RECIPES` that are not `unknown` for the worker (ones it cannot afford yet are offered with the reason, and the order waits); walk spots found with `nearestFree`. A form with nothing to offer cannot be issued and says why (`BUILD A STORAGE CHEST FIRST`).
  - *Commands.* `giveWorkerOrder` (= `setWorkerOrder`, with its refusal said in words by `sim/jobs.ts: orderRefusal`, which mirrors `setWorkerOrder` / `assignJob` check for check: not a worker, out of reach, no clear line, unknown or invalid order with the argument named, capability missing, busy with its own task) and `controlWorker` (`pause` / `resume` / `cancel` through `pauseJob`, `resumeJob`, `setWorkerOrder(null)`, behind the same reach and sight rule). An order given to a switched-off worker is kept and waits for a core.
  - *The core.* The first row calls `orderCoreSwap` with the action `coreActionFor` picks, exactly as `C` does. The golem does the swap with the world running, so the screen steps aside (`pending`) and the host reopens it when the reach is done, as for the crafting bench. The reach that opened the screen is still finishing beneath it, so `orderCoreSwap` no longer counts a fired `manageActor` reach as busy. The cycle (pack -> golem -> pack -> guardian, a full pack drops the core) is unchanged.
- *Text.* Strings are short, uppercase and font-only. `BLOCK_TEXT` reasons are at most 15 characters so `BLOCKED: ` fits beside them.
- *Tests.* `tests/storageScreen.test.ts` (open, both ways, stack / partial / full / empty / critical, cross-pane navigation at every tier, close, gone or out of reach, save and load with items in the chest, determinism, a text lint); `tests/manageScreen.test.ts` (idle, off, working, paused, blocked, done and waiting states; the core row against `orderCoreSwap` on identical worlds, including the full-pack drop and the lingering opening reach; gather and craft orders equal to the builders and run end to end; go to; pause / resume / cancel; refusal table; only-valid-targets; a save mid-job; a lint of every view and row); `tests/workerHost.test.ts` (the real host: E opens the right screen, keys drive both, the core swap hands the golem its reach and brings the screen back, forged worker events and wrong-screen player events open nothing). `tests/support/screenHost.ts` is a headless `UiHost` with a recorder for the font's text calls: `assertTextFits` checks font glyphs, the panel bounds and that no two strings overlap; `tests/support/workerFixture.ts` is the shared foundation fixture. They render with `tests/support/uiSnapshot.ts`'s pixel canvas at 320x180 (PNGs land in `.test-out/shots`).

### Vision and analysis
`PlayerInput.toggleScan` toggles `Actor.mode`; the host forwards Z, and **the sim** chooses a scan task before the ordinary prop interaction. Buildable furniture is a scan-only target: it is not a normal interactable, so E on it does nothing unless vision/scan mode is on, and it can still be targeted when it holds no item: `scanTargets` ignores `taken`, which on a buildable only means the item resting on it was picked up. (Before v12 it skipped such props, so the workbench without its chisel and the library table without its Primer could no longer be scanned, although vision still marked them pending.) Analysis revalidates the prop, reach and sight, emits an event and a short line, and grants `analysis.<key>` for every plan the prop's kind teaches (`buildRows`: recipes whose `prop` is that kind, or that name it in `learnFrom`). Taking a buildable apart reads its plans too (`learnPlans`, for actors that can scan), so no plan is lost with the last example of its kind. `Recipe.analysis` is optional: omit it for a plan that cannot be discovered in an area. Catalog status, the build effect and the menu's reason all read one gate, `planLock` (`knowledge` or `analysis`). For an analysis lock the reason names what to scan and that vision is Z ("SCAN A TABLE IN VISION (Z)", at most 36 characters); missing knowledge still reads "UNKNOWN RECIPE - NOT YET LEARNED".

Highlight priorities, colours and each category's hologram effect (`radiant`, `wireframe`, `signal`, `blueprint`) are data in `content/vision.ts`. `visionTargets` is the read-only world projection: each target also carries `source` (prop / loose / actor / tile, so presentation can find its art) and `analysis` (`pending` while any of a prop's plans is unread, `done` once all are, absent if there is nothing to read), from the same flags `applyAnalysis` grants. Interactive tiles are cached on `Area.revision` instead of scanning the grid every frame.

`view/vision.ts: VisionView` is the presentation. The host only calls `update` (in `updateWorldFx`, after the golem's light is traced), `draw` (after lighting compose) and `drawHud`, and maps the returned `VisionCue`s (`on`, `off`, `ping`, `lock`) to sound and particles. Nothing in it blocks input: the sim's mode flips instantly and the overlay catches up.
- **Sight.** Targets show only within the golem's light radius (`Light.rad`, so a rune of glow widens it) *and* line of sight, read from that light's traced `vis` grid. Wall-mounted props also count the floor tile in front of their wall.
- **Switching.** On: a sweep front runs out from the golem in 0.3 s. The world desaturates (a `saturation` blend) and dims behind it through a cached, Bayer-dithered sight mask (light inside the radius, heavy outside), and each target pings in (a white flash and a category-pitched tick) as the front reaches it. Off: holograms squash to a line and the ring pulls back into the eye in about 0.16 s. While the mode holds, a dashed ring marks the edge of sight and a faint re-scan pulse passes every few seconds.
- **Holograms.** These are built from the sprite's own pixels: the silhouette's outer outline and inner edge, the sprite's internal shading edges, and its luminance. They are baked once per (sprite composite, category, state) into small frame strips and drawn with `lighter` (fill) plus `source-over` (outline). NPCs are procedural, so they are re-traced each frame through `drawActor` into a scratch canvas. Rune circles use their carved line art. Highlights fade toward the edge of sight; one standing in front of the golem fades so the golem stays readable, and the golem gets a faint scanner outline of its own.
- **Analysis state.** `pending` targets are fully animated and carry a pulsing diamond. `done` targets drop to a single still, dim outline (no fill, no motion) with a check mark. When a target flips from pending to done, the `lock` cue fires on the same frame the effect does: a flash, an outline echo, motes and a chime. A scan in progress draws a beam reading the target top to bottom up to the task's fire point, with a dotted data stream back to the eye.

Actor drawing was extracted into `view/actors.ts` to keep these hooks from growing the host. It picks a painter by the ActorDef's art key: `NPC_ART` (guardian-shaped poses), `BODY_ART` (`workerArt.ts`: bodies whose pose carries power, activity, walking and task motion; the worker golem), the golem, or the test-only human fixture. Every path draws both passes, here and when vision retraces actors each frame into its scratch canvas. The old scaled-golem variant and `ActorDef.art.scale` are gone. The golem and guardian goldens are unchanged.

### Production rollout (v12)
The foundations are part of the real game now:
- **Build plans need analysis**, except the torch post and the barricade: nothing like them stands in any production area, so a gate would lock them forever. The gated plans, and what to scan: wall torch (any wall torch), brazier (a brazier; it still also needs `know.masonry`, which no book grants yet), crafting bench (the artificery's workbench), table, chair and wall banner (their own kinds), and the storage chest (`Recipe.learnFrom`: the treasury's looted chests, since no storage chest stands anywhere). The flow is: read the Primer (`know.build`), then scan things before building them. The tutorial's critical path (arm, key, guardian core, chisel, schematic, four glyphs, the circle) builds, crafts and analyzes nothing, and no rule or story step reads a gate (tested). Crafting is never analysis-gated: the ruby chain needs only the station's knowledge, and since dismantling reads a plan, taking the only workbench apart can't lose the bench either.
- **The storage chest** is a buildable 1-tile, 32-slot `storage_chest` (4 planks) that shares only the old chest's art. Looted dungeon `chest` props are never buildable and never in `AUTHORED_SALVAGE`. E on a chest opens the storage screen (see "Worker management and storage screens").
- **The ruby chain**: the crown (loose in the throne hall, behind the cracked wall, so after the iron arm) -> UNMAKE BROKEN CROWN at a crafting bench -> the HIGH-GRADE RUBY -> RUBY GOLEM CORE, carved with the rune chisel -> the dormant worker golem. The ruby keeps its internal id `large_ruby`: it only ever existed through debug fixtures, so a rename would only add a migration. There is one crown, so the whole game has one ruby and one core.
- **The dormant worker golem** (`npc.worker.artificery`, `worker_golem`) is a spawn row in the dungeon builder with no NPCS row, so it has no dialogue: E on it is `manageActor` (it has the `worker` capability). Vision shows it violet ("someone"). It is parked in the alcove between the forge and the cradle, against the north wall. That keeps row 38 (start, cradle, forge, workbench) and the exit corridor open, which a `sim/reach.ts` check proves, as for the guardian. It is in frame during the opening, left of the waking maintainer and clear of the diagnostic log and the bars, so the opening needed no change.
- **The screens at the three old seams** (now filled): `manageActor` opens the management screen, `openStorage` the storage screen, and the core slot seam (`orderCoreSwap` / `slotCore` / `coreSlotted`, see "Body power") is called by both the C key and the management screen.

**Foundation fixture.** Open with `?debug`, then call `window.dustwake.foundations()` to run the deterministic two-area gather/travel/deposit fixture. `window.dustwake.order(actorId, order)` uses the same walk-up command as a future order screen. The existing save/load hooks work mid-job. `registerFoundations()` registers only the debug areas and their rune link, before loading a fixture save. It no longer touches a catalog: the chest, ruby and inverse rows are production rows, and `content/foundations.ts` is gone. The fixture builders live in `world/foundationAreas.ts`, have stable ids and use no randomness.

**v10 migration.** `MIGRATIONS[9]` adds actor definitions/upgrades, empty body-power sockets, normal interaction mode and empty jobs. Only the player's old body flags become its upgrades; NPCs cannot inherit them. Existing packs, gear and in-flight work are preserved. A player with `know.build` gets the frozen v9 discoverable-plan analysis keys, not a blanket bypass that would unlock future content. Existing relic credit is recovered from the pack and authored pickup diffs. Storage inventories are diffed for both built and authored props. Browser storage defaults moved to `view/persistence.ts`; `world/save.ts` accepts an injected structural storage interface and has no DOM dependency.

**v12 migration.** `MIGRATIONS[11]` grants `analysis.<id>` to a save with `know.build` for exactly the plans gated in v12 that v10/v11 let it build. It is a frozen literal list (wall torch, brazier, crafting bench, table, chair, wall banner), never derived from `RECIPES`, so the storage chest and any later plan stay locked. A save without `know.build` gets nothing, and analysis flags a save already has are kept. Actors live on the World, so a dungeon visited before v12 never re-runs its spawns: the migration adds the dormant worker, following the v4 guardian precedent. It matches by id, because `MIGRATIONS[4]` loops the live builder's spawn list and so already gives the worker to v4 saves. If the player stands where it goes, it takes a second spot (the artificery's south-west corner), so no body starts inside another.

### Area: dimensions, identity, change propagation
- An `AreaData` carries its own `id`, `w` and `h`. Lighting (`computeVis`), background baking, collision, the camera clamp and the schematic all read those instead of global `MW/MH`. Adding the surface means adding a builder to `world/areas.ts`. `enterArea()` moves an actor between areas, and `Game.syncView()` rebuilds the view when the player's area changes. The tests travel to a fixture area of a different size.
- Props have **stable ids** (`kind@tx,ty`, or explicit ones like `ped0`), and loose items do too. Saves and prop-owned lights refer to these ids.
- **All mutation goes through `Area` methods.** `revision` counts all changes; `layoutRevision` tracks collision/layout, `interactionRevision` tracks interactables/light ownership, and `storageRevision` tracks contents. Storage deposits, fractional harvest progress and clock animation do not invalidate layout or interaction caches. Tile and decal changes also publish a padded dirty rectangle to subscribers.
  - Interactables and light reconciliation cache on `interactionRevision`; body-specific nav grids cache on `layoutRevision`. Save restoration and ritual changes also use Area methods.
  - `AreaView` merges dirty rects and catches up once per frame. It re-bakes only that region and re-traces visibility only for lights whose window overlaps it.
  - The old code instead called `bakeRegion` + `refreshLightsNear` + `buildInters` by hand at each call site, with hard-coded tile coordinates. The new re-bake region is always a superset of the old one, so the pixels are identical.
- Lights are **reconciled from world state**: area lights (prop-owned ones follow their prop), glowing items on props, socket lights and the core light. The registry is insertion-ordered, so lights accumulate in the same order as before.

### Work: tasks and effects as data
The old `Action` held a closure. Now a `Task` is `{kind, dur, effect, target}`, and a fire point per kind comes from `content/tasks.ts`. `tickTask` advances any actor's task and applies its `Effect` through `applyEffect`, which is the single place interactions change the world. Every tutorial interaction runs this way. Because tasks are plain data:
- in-progress work is saved;
- any actor can perform it (`stepWorld` ticks non-player actors; tested with a fixture actor);
- actors have a queue (`enqueueTask`). The tutorial never queues, but the tests do.

Which task an interaction starts is decided by **rules in `content/interactions.ts`**: the first rule whose `{items, flags}` requirement is met wins. Delayed consequences, like a container's item arriving 0.25s later, use `schedule()` on **simulation time** instead of `setTimeout`.

"Repair this tile" and "build a torch here" become new `Effect` ops plus rules. Multi-step orders use the job layer above; a job board and automatic work allocation are deliberately not built yet.

### Attack: the two-arm slam
Attacking and interacting are separate inputs: `PlayerInput.use` (E / Enter) and `PlayerInput.attack` (Space). Only the play screen maps Space to `attack`; menus, the pack and dialogue keep their own keys (Space still confirms there). `stepPlayer` handles `use` first; if that started nothing, `attack` calls `sim/interact.ts: startSlam`. The slam needs only what interacting needs, an actor with no task in progress, so it works anywhere, facing anything, with or without the iron arm, and it is unavailable exactly when interacting is (busy, or an overlay on top, which stops the play screen updating). It is an area attack, not an aimed one: nothing is chosen when it starts (the task is always `SLAM_TASK`, `slam`, 1.1 s, effect `{op: "slam"}`, targeting just the ground at the strike point). It fires at `SLAM_HIT` (half way): it emits `slam` at the strike point (`sim/tasks.ts: strikePoint`, where the fists meet the ground just in front of the actor, between the two arms, not the golem's centre) and then hits everything slammable within **`SLAM_RADIUS`** (`content/tasks.ts`, 20 px, a little over a tile) of that point. `slamReach` finds them at impact, in the world as it is then (so nothing needs re-validating, and a slam saved mid-swing lands correctly): tiles whose `TILE_INTERACT` kind has `SLAM_RULES` and that have any part of their square within the radius, a run of tiles that act as one thing (the cracked wall) counting once, nearest first. For each, the first rule in **`SLAM_RULES`** (`content/interactions.ts`) whose requirement is met gives the effect applied to it. The radius is sized to the dust ring the impact throws up (it drifts a median ~18 px, three quarters of it within ~24 px), since the blow is not that strong. The only row today is `cracked`: with `golem.arm`, `breakTile` (the old smash's effect, so `wallBroken` brings the same smash sound, shake, flying debris and smoke); without it, `withstand`, which changes nothing and emits `withstood` (a heavy impact sound, a smaller shake, chips and dust, and the debris sprite jolts and flashes) plus a hint. **Harvestables are slammed too.** Standing trees, rocks and logs (props with a `content/harvest.ts` row) are not slammable *tiles* and have no `SLAM_RULES` row: at impact, `sim/harvesting.ts: harvestReach` finds every one whose footprint (its collision box, else its anchor) has any part within `SLAM_RADIUS` of the strike point, nearest first (ties by id), and the slam gives each one blow of work through `harvestProp` (the one place a blow is applied, shared with the workers' `harvest` effect). Several in reach are all harvested by the same slam, each emitting its own `harvestHit` and paying its own yield; the `slam` event's `hit` is true if any slammable or harvestable was in reach. Anything else, or nothing (including a depleted stump), just takes the blow: the slam plays in full and only the ground thud and dust follow. `E` on a cracked wall now pushes it (the old shudder) and says to use Space; no `RULES` row breaks it any more. The old one-arm `smash` task kind is kept, unused by rules, so saved work in progress still plays out. The harvest `punch` task and `harvest` effect are unchanged for workers' jobs (they walk to a chosen prop and punch it); the golem itself no longer has a harvest interaction. No save version bump: tasks and effects are data, and the new kind and ops only add values.

Art: `GolemPose.slam` (-1..1 for both arms: overhead, rest, fists in the ground) and `dip` (body px). `golem.ts: slamPose(k)` is the curve: wind-up, a short gather at the top, a fast drop that lands exactly at `SLAM_HIT` (the fire point, so the impact frame, the effect, the shake and the dust happen in the same frame), a hold while the body sinks with the blow, then recovery. Arms are drawn as outlined segments from shoulder to fist, so both can point overhead or into the ground in any facing. Facing away, the arms come down along the body's sides (the fists land in front of the golem, behind its body from here). Without the iron arm, the broken stub swings through the same arc, a third as long with its wires trailing, and sparks on impact. Poses without `slam` draw exactly as before (the legacy golden).

### Inner monologue and the opening
The player golem thinks short lines. A thought is not dialogue (no box, no portrait, nothing pauses) and not a note or toast (those are the game talking to the player). **Lines are data** (`content/monologue.ts: THOUGHTS`): `text`, optional event triggers `on: {event, match?}` (a `SimEvent` type plus field equality), `when` (a `Requirement` on the thinker), `if` (named conditions), `once` (default true), `cooldown` (recurring lines), and the presentation hints `tone` (`status` / `warn` / `void`) and `pace`. Adding a line is a row. Adding a trigger means writing `on` against an existing event. Adding a condition is a row in `sim/monologue.ts: THOUGHT_CONDITIONS`, a predicate over `{w, a, event?, target?}`. The `thoughtSubject` helper there resolves which actor the event is about.

**The sim decides when** (`sim/monologue.ts`), in one of three ways: `think(w, actor, id)` (scripts and cutscenes), the `{op: "think", line}` Effect (interaction rules, schedules and dialogue beats, whose conditions see the task target), and `reactToEvents(w)`. That last one offers every pending event to the `on` triggers, in emission order then row order, and skips other actors' events and thoughts themselves. It runs at the end of `stepPlayer` / `stepWorld` and at the top of `Game.drainEvents`, so events emitted by paused-world commands (reading a book, the pack) are seen too. Which events it has already offered is a transient `WeakSet` keyed by event object, so the World needs no extra field. Only the player thinks. A fired line is recorded and emits `{t: "thought", actor, line}`. **State is saved** in `World.monologue`: `said` holds the one-shot ids in order, and `ready` holds the sim time at which each recurring line may fire again. Both are part of save **v11**. `MIGRATIONS[10]` adds an empty record. It doesn't mark the opening's lines as said: a loaded save never replays the opening, and the opening is the only thing that fires them.

**Presentation decides how it looks** (`ui/monologue.ts`, no DOM). The host pushes `thought` events into a `MonologueLog`, steps it in `updateWorldFx` (scenes and animating overlays; it waits behind the book) and draws it in `renderHud`. It is a small diagnostic log in the top-left corner, in the bitmap font. Each line has a dim `>` prompt and types one line at a time at 30 characters per second (times `pace`) behind a block cursor, with one unresolved glyph flickering at its edge. A faint tick plays per character (`sfx.thoughtTick`), and a double blip as each line starts. A finished line holds for 2.6 s plus 0.05 s per character, then fades, and the rows below slide up. A line ending in `...` is a running process: its dots cycle while it is newest, and it dims once something follows it. `void` lines type slower, waver slightly, and keep their cursor blinking longer. All layout and pacing numbers are in `MONO`. A pinned log doesn't age, which is what the opening uses.

Rows today: the four opening lines (`intro.*`, fired by the cutscene) and `vision.analyzed` ("OBJECT ANALYZED.", on `analyzed`, recurring with a 1 s cooldown because one analysis can grant several plans; the existing `ANALYZED: <NAME>` note stays). The power lines ship (v12). `golem.dormant` ("INTEGRITY INTACT, MISSING POWER SOURCE.") fires on `manageActor` with `subjectNoPower`: E on the dormant worker golem. It is only `manageActor` because `subjectNoPower` holds for the unpowered guardian too, and E on the guardian talks. `golem.underpowered` ("INSUFFICIENT POWER SOURCE.", `warn`) fires with `subjectWeakPower` on `manageActor`, `talkedTo` and `coreSlotted` (inserted): as the ruby core goes into the guardian, then on every talk, in every talk state, including the idle line once its story is done (`dialogue` fires only when a segment plays, so a trigger on it alone went silent). Both recur with a 2 s cooldown. The dialogue content is unchanged. Learning from a book is a note, not a thought (the golem doesn't say "you"): `content/flags.ts: LEARNED_TEXT` maps the `learned` event to its text, so the Primer shows **YOU LEARNED TO BUILD.** once (the `learn` effect only emits the first time, and the flag is saved).

**The opening** is extracted from the host. Its script is data (`content/cutscenes.ts: INTRO`): a fade-in, the starting body tracks, and ordered beats. Each beat starts `wait` seconds after the previous one, so retiming one beat shifts only what follows. A beat may fire cues, `think` a line, and set the `core` (`off` / `boot` / `on` / `diagnose`), `eye` or `body` track, with `over` for ramps. A track holds until a later beat changes it. The single last beat is `control`. `ui/cutscene.ts` is the stateless runner (`timeline`, `beatsCrossed(t0, t]`, `trackAt`, `controlAt`), so a replay always starts clean. `screens/intro.ts` interprets the script through `CutsceneHost` (UiHost plus the golem's look, sparks, shake, `worldFrame`, the letterbox, `startPlay`, `renderScene`). Its cue table `CUTSCENE_CUES` is one row per cue. The first eight beats are the original awakening at the original times: sparks 0.8, boot 1.2 to 2.6, eye 2.9 to 3.5, rise 3.6 to 5.0, thud 4.2. The new beats: "ASSESSING..." at 5.0, with the core in stepped, irregular pulses and soft data chirps for 2.4 s. A settling two-tone at 7.4. Then the list, as a list: "MILD DAMAGE DETECTED." (7.75), "MISSING LEFT ARM." (8.85, the torn shoulder spits sparks) and, after a longer pause, "PRIME DIRECTIVE: NONE." (10.1, slow, over a hollow low swell). **Control is handed over at 11.9 s** (`controlAt(INTRO)`), after one held beat once the last line has finished typing. The cinematic letterbox shown during the whole intro slides away there, a soft `online` tone plays, and the pinned list is released to fade (oldest first). The play screen takes input from the next frame; until then the player is never stepped. R at the chapter card that closes the first crossing (`wakeAgain`: `reset` + `introUi.start()`) replays the opening on a fresh world. Esc skips the opening: `skip()` thinks the remaining lines through the sim, clears the log and hands over through the same function the control beat uses. Loading a save goes through `Game.resume`, straight to play.

### Time
`world.time` advances only in `stepWorld`, i.e. only while the world simulates (overlays pause it, as before). Scheduled effects and the ritual charge run on this clock. `Game.t` is a separate real clock for animation, so flames still flicker while the pack is open, exactly as before. The host still passes the browser's variable `dt` (clamped to 0.05s, unchanged); see "Deferred".

### Determinism
The World owns a `SeededRng` whose state is saved. The puzzle solution and the debris scattered when the wall breaks come from it; both used `Math.random` before. Each new session draws a fresh seed, so players still get a different solution each time, with the same distribution. Presentation randomness (particles, flicker, shake, rumble timing, the golem's sputter) stays on `Math.random` on purpose. A test checks that the same seed plus the same inputs produce identical worlds, including after a save/load.

### Progression
`world.flags` holds shared progression: `know.schematic`, `know.build`, `tutorial.insight`, `golem.eye` (the guardian's eye repair), rune activation, analysis and relic discovery. Body upgrades (`golem.arm`, `golem.slot.*`) moved to actors in v10; even a legacy `Requirement.flags` body name is checked against the actor being tested, not the player. NPC story state is namespaced by actor id: `npc.guardian.awake`, `met`, `coreGiven`, `farewell`. The ritual's `core`, `carved` and `sockets` describe a world structure and live on its Area.

### Content as data
Items (`ITEMS`: name, tags, description, glyph index, equip behaviour, `wear` (which gear slots take it), `worn` effects, glow, show-on-prop, reading screen), props (`PROPS`: collision box, wall-mounted, alternate-sprite rule, emitted light, fire/embers, item offset), tiles (solidity, opacity, what interacting does, decals they leave), glyphs, task timings and interaction rules all live in `content/`. Sprites stay procedural code in `art/`, in two registries of draw functions (`PROP_ART` keyed by prop kind, `ITEM_ART` keyed by item id), with the albedo/emissive split unchanged. `art/` is split by what it draws: `tiles.ts` (background bake and tile sprites), `decals.ts`, `props.ts` (registry and `propSprite`; draw functions in `propsSurface.ts` and `propsDungeon.ts`, merged in that order), `items.ts`, plus `palette.ts` and `common.ts` (the one shared sprite cache and the registry types). Dependencies point one way: props/items/tiles -> common -> palette, tiles -> decals. `tests/art.test.ts` renders every pre-registry sprite and the dungeon bake through a recording fake canvas and checks the draw-call trace hash against the one captured from the original `switch` code. `tests/artRegistry.test.ts` does the same for every `PROP_ART`/`ITEM_ART` entry, the tile sprites, glyph patterns and the surface bake, with goldens captured from the single-file `art.ts` before the split.

### Screens
`ui/screens.ts` is a small stack, replacing the `State` string. Only the top screen gets `update`, and therefore input. Rendering starts at the highest opaque screen and draws upward. Scene screens (intro, play, tele) step the world themselves; overlays (pack, schematic) pause it just by sitting on top. A management screen is one more `Screen` pushed onto the stack. The `dialogue` screen is such an overlay (see "NPCs and dialogue"). Screens that don't need the game class live in `screens/` and talk to the host through `ui/kit.ts: UiHost` (contexts, world, input, sfx, notes, toast, plus `read(item)`, `boostEye`, and `animate(dt)` for overlays that keep the scene alive): the build menu and tools (`buildMenu.ts`), the crafting bench (`crafting.ts`), the pack (`pack.ts`), books (`book.ts`), the schematic (`schematic.ts`, which owns the rendered plan) and dialogue (`dialogue.ts`). The title and chapter cards are pure render functions (`cards.ts`); their update logic (screen changes) stays in the host. The host keeps input, the stack, camera, particles, audio, event reactions, the scene screens (play, tele, arrive) and world rendering. The opening is `screens/intro.ts` behind `CutsceneHost` (see "Inner monologue and the opening"). Cross-screen moves go through the host (the pack opens a book with `host.read`), so screens never import each other.

### Two render layers and zoom
The page stacks two canvases with the same display rect. The **UI layer** stays at the base 320x180: HUD, notes, toasts, every overlay, the title and chapter cards draw there exactly as before (`Game.ctx`). The **world layer** (`Game.wctx`) holds the lit scene and anything in world space (the interaction marker, the build ghost). Its size is the current view: `view/zoom.ts` maps a zoom level z to `(320z) x (180z)`, snapped to multiples of 16x9 so it is always integer and 16:9. One world pixel is one canvas pixel at every zoom, and the browser scales both layers with nearest-neighbour (`image-rendering: pixelated`), so there is no resampling blur. `Game.setZoom` reallocates the scene, emissive and lighting buffers only when the snapped size changes. Camera clamps, culling windows, the chunk-bake `prepare()` window, light tracing, particle spawn areas and the surface arrival use `vw/vh`, not the constants. Zoom is a presentation preference: it is not in the sim or the save. Before `golem.eye` it is fixed at 1x. The eye repair eases it out to 1.5x over about a second. After that the wheel steps 1x/1.5x/2x on the play screen only (non-passive listener, so the page never scrolls). A world loaded with `golem.eye` starts at 1.5x. At 2x on the 96x72 surface the first frame bakes 24 chunks (about 110 ms with a cold JIT, behind the arrival fade) and lighting costs about 5 ms per frame (`npm test` prints it).

### Save format
`serialize(world)` writes JSON: the world's scalar fields, flags, the schedule, actors (tasks included), and for each visited area a **diff against its deterministic builder** (changed tiles, prop state, taken loose items, decals added during play, ritual state). A mid-tutorial save is about 4 KB. `migrate()` runs `MIGRATIONS[n]` (v n → n+1) and rejects saves from newer versions. **v5** (NPCs): `MIGRATIONS[4]` adds the dungeon's NPC actors (the guardian, dormant). If the v4 chest had been opened (or a core is in a pack, on the ground or seated in the circle), it sets `npc.guardian.coreGiven`, so the guardian still tells its story and mends the eye but never hands over a second core. Prop `open` is now diffed against the builder, because props can start open. **v6** (building overhaul): areas gain `propsRemoved`, the ids of builder-placed props that were deconstructed. `diffArea` derives it (base ids missing from the area) and `restoreArea` removes them again, so they never come back. Built props carry `recipe`. `MIGRATIONS[5]` adds empty `propsRemoved` and rewrites pending build work from the old recipe id `torch` to `torch_post`. **v7**: the crafting bench no longer has its own prop kind; `MIGRATIONS[6]` turns built `craftbench` props into `workbench` (ids and the recipe id `craftbench` are unchanged, so pending work and favorites still match). **v8** (gear): actors gain `gear`. The basket used to be installed on pickup as the world flag `golem.basket`; `MIGRATIONS[7]` removes the flag and puts the basket in the player's back slot (same 48-slot tier, the pack untouched). A save taken mid-attach (basket picked up, `equip` task still running) also ends with it worn, and the stale task is dropped. **v9** (materials and gear slots): built props record what they cost (`refund`) and taking one apart gives exactly that back, so rebalancing a recipe never changes what a standing prop refunds. `MIGRATIONS[8]` stamps props built before this with the v8 cost of their recipe (frozen inside the migration, falling back to the prop's kind for saves that never recorded the recipe), and drops build work still pending for a recipe whose cost changed (a build only takes its materials when it fires, so dropping loses nothing). Gear needs no change: `Actor.gear` only gains keys, and slot unlocks are world flags (`golem.slot.*`). **v10** is described under "Actor foundations". **v11** (inner monologue): the save gains `monologue` (`said`, `ready`); `MIGRATIONS[10]` adds it empty. **v12** (production rollout) is described under "Production rollout (v12)": `MIGRATIONS[11]` grants the frozen legacy plans to saves that knew building, and adds the dormant worker golem once. Rule: any schema change bumps `SAVE_VERSION` and adds a migration. Area builders must stay deterministic, and ids must stay stable.

**v14** (the entrance): no field changed shape. A dig site's progress is its prop's existing `dmg` and `open`, an opened entrance is a world flag, and the stair it clears is ordinary tile and removed-prop diffs. `MIGRATIONS[13]` exists for the layout: the artificery and the armory were refurnished and iron veins stand on the surface, and a body saved where one of those now stands could never move again. Any actor whose feet box overlaps one of the new footprints (a frozen list, with the two rooms' floors frozen beside it so the step out is never a step into a wall) is set down beside it. `settle` (the load-from-storage guard) now also counts solid props, not only masonry, for the player. See "The entrance update (v14)".

Player-facing save/load is deliberately minimal: `K` on the play screen writes the one localStorage slot (`saveToStorage`), and `L` on the play or title screen loads it (`loadFromStorage` -> `Game.resume`). It is the same format and the same functions as the debug hooks; no slots, no autosave, no menu. Open the game with `?debug` to get `window.dustwake.save() / load() / serialize()`.

### Inventory
`Actor.inventory` is an ordered array of slots (`{item, n}` or `null`). Items declare `stack` (max per slot; absent = unique) and `droppable` in `ITEMS`. **Capacity is derived, never stored**: each `ActorDef.inventory` declares its base size and optional `{requires, slots}` tiers; the highest satisfied tier wins. The player's row reuses `CAPACITY_TIERS` (8 / 16 with its own arm upgrade / 48 wearing the basket); the lesser golem and human fixture have 8, the guardian 0. Tiers read actor upgrades and worn gear, never "owns item X". The iron arm still installs on pickup (attach animation, never a pack slot), but its upgrade belongs to the actor picking it up. `reconcileCapacity` runs for every actor, NPCs included: growth appends empty slots; a shrink repacks then drops overflow next to that actor. Items are never deleted.

Anything that can't be stored lands on the ground as a loose stack (`giveItem`). Dropping (`Q`) is the `drop` effect: it places the item deterministically on the tile in front of the actor, else its own tile, else a fixed ring order around it (`dropSpot`). Same-item stacks on one spot merge. Loose items created in play get ids `drop<n>` from a saved per-area counter. Tutorial-critical items (chisel, key, core, glyphs, schematic) are `droppable: false`. All actors have blocking bodies, and drops avoid the other actors. Loose items never interact with ritual sockets (sockets only accept glyphs through the picker), which is tested.

### Gear (slots, locks and equippable runes)
`Actor.gear` maps a body slot to the item worn there. **The slots are data** (`content/gear.ts: GEAR_SLOT_DEFS`): `back`, `head`, `armR`, `armL`, the `core` attachment mount and `rune0`..`rune3`. A slot row supplies vocabulary and the maintainer's default hints/locks. Each `ActorDef` selects its `gearSlots` and optional `gearUnlocks`; the maintainer copies those legacy locks, a human does not inherit its damaged arm, and another body can lock any slot with its own requirement. **A slot is usable only if that actor's definition includes it and its requirement is met.** Body unlock requirements use `actorFlags`; granting the player's extra slots never opens another actor's. The lesser golem only has `rune0`, even if someone grants it every slot upgrade. The player's initial usable/locked slots and paper doll are unchanged.

Decisions on the existing design:
- **Arms.** The golem's left arm is the damaged side (the broken stub the iron arm replaces: `golem.ts` draws it on screen-right facing down); the right is the sound stone arm. The right arm slot is usable from the start. The iron arm stays an *equip* item (`golem.arm`, an installed body part, never a slot item). Since v14 it opens the left arm slot: the slot's unlock is `any: [golem.arm, golem.slot.armL]` (`Requirement.any`), so it is usable exactly while the arm is on, or while its own upgrade flag is set (the debug kit; a proper arm later). Nothing is stored for it, so there is nothing to migrate, and a head worn there goes back to the pack if the arm ever comes off (`reconcileGear`). Arm slots take tool heads (see "The entrance update (v14)").
- **The aether core** stays a plain, undroppable pack item consumed by the ritual circle (`placeCore` reads the pack): not slot-aware, so the tutorial can't be broken by where it sits. The `core` slot is a separate mount for attachments to the chest core; the aether core item has no `wear`, so it can't go there.
- **Capacity tiers** are unchanged: `{worn: ["basket"]}` reads any slot.

Items opt in with `wear: {kind, slot?}`: `kind` picks the slots that take it (`slotsOfKind`), `slot` restricts to one. `sim/gear.ts` holds the rules:
- **Wear** (`wearCheck` / `applyWear`, effect `wear {slot, into?}`): the item goes into `into` if given and usable, else the first empty usable slot of its kind, else it swaps with the first usable one (what was worn there goes to the freed pack slot). No usable slot of its kind refuses (`noSlot`), so a locked slot never receives anything. Capacity follows at once (grows by appending empty slots; a swap to something granting less must still fit).
- **Take off** (`takeOffCheck` / `applyTakeOff`, effect `takeOff`): allowed only if `slotsNeeded(pack + the item itself) <= capacity without it`. `slotsNeeded` merges units of the same item up to their stack size. A refusal emits a message (a shrinking pack says how many slots to free; otherwise the pack is just full) and changes nothing; `repackInto` fits a shrinking pack without dropping anything.
- **Locked slots hold nothing.** `reconcileGear` runs every world step: whatever is worn in a slot that isn't usable (a flag cleared, the debug toggle, an odd save) goes back to the pack, and if that is full the overflow lands on the ground. Nothing is deleted.
- **Effects only while worn.** An item's `worn` row (`ItemDef.worn`: `speed`, `light`, `mark`) counts only in usable slots (`activeGear`). `speedMultiplier(w, a)` is `1 + sum(speed)` and `stepPlayer` multiplies the locomotion speed by it (the sim, not the host). `golemGlow(w, a)` is the golem's standard light (`GOLEM_LIGHT`, formerly hard-coded in the host) or the widest worn light; `light.ts: applyGlow` puts it on the golem's `Light` each frame and invalidates the traced visibility when the radius changes. The host only forwards. More runes are new `ITEMS` rows.
- **Runes (items).** Two exist, both carved from cut stone: *Rune of Swift* (`worn.speed`) and *Rune of Glow* (`worn.light`: white, radius 176 against 108, strength 1.25). "Increases speed by 150%" is read literally as +150%, i.e. **2.5x**, the same convention the repo's debug super-speed uses ("200% faster = 3x"); it is one constant, `RUNE_SWIFT_SPEED_BONUS` (content/gear.ts), and the light is `RUNE_GLOW_LIGHT`.
- **The pack screen** (`screens/pack.ts`) is an RPG paper doll: the golem figure in a niche with all nine slots around it and the pack grid beside it, a description panel under both. Slot states are drawn distinctly: empty (a faint silhouette of what it takes, `art/slots.ts`), filled (the item in a warm frame), locked (greyed, hatched, a padlock; the description panel says why). The cursor is a pack cell or a doll slot (`PackUi.cursor` / `PackUi.slot`) and moves spatially (`neighbor`: the nearest slot in the pressed direction, preferring what is lined up); right off the doll's right-hand edge enters the pack, left off the pack's first column lands on the doll level with the cursor. `E` on a wearable item calls `commands.wearFromSlot` (the cursor follows it onto its slot), `E` on a worn slot calls `commands.takeOffGear`, `E` on a locked slot only thuds; the world is paused, so these apply at once like `dropFromSlot`. The sim emits `gearOn` / `gearOff`; the host answers with a soft creak (a chime for runes) and a short note.
- **Golem art.** `GolemPose.runes` (from `sim/gear.ts: runeMarks`: one entry per rune slot) draws a 3x3 glowing sigil per worn rune on the pauldrons and lower torso (front/back) or the side of the body, after everything else and offset by the body bob; the basket is drawn from `GolemPose.back` as before in all four facings. Poses without `runes` draw exactly as before: the legacy golden is untouched.

### Rune network
(The teleportation runes. Not the equippable runes of the gear slots above.)
`content/runes.ts` declares `RUNE_NODES` (a rune prop with a stable id in some area, its `rune.<id>.active` flag, and an arrival offset/facing) and `RUNE_LINKS` (declared once, bidirectional unless `oneWay`; `awakens` lets arriving wake a dormant destination). Interacting with an active rune runs a `channel` task whose `travel` effect resolves the link, places the actor at the destination prop + offset (nudged to the nearest free spot, deterministically), and calls `enterArea()`. The end of the tutorial uses the same path: standing on the charged circle starts the long first `channel` (one-shot, guarded by `tutorial.done`), and arriving wakes the surface rune. The dungeon circle is a `runesite` prop (`rune.dungeon`); its flag follows the ritual's active state.

### Harvesting and building
`content/harvest.ts`: hp and yields per prop kind. Harvestables are deliberately **not interactables** (`deriveInteractables` skips them while standing, there is no `RULES.harvest`, and `visionTargets` doesn't count them, so the golem's vision doesn't highlight them and `E` does nothing on them). The golem harvests with the attack: the slam harvests every harvestable within `SLAM_RADIUS` of the fists (see the attack section above). A worker's gather job still walks to one prop and punches it with the `harvest` effect. Either way a blow is one `harvestProp` call (`sim/tasks.ts`): `Area.damageProp` stores cumulative work on the prop (saved, any actor can contribute). The legacy `perHit` name now means yield per unit of work, not per animation: pay the difference of the floored cumulative entitlements before and after the blow, clamp work to hp, and pay `final` once. A half-power worker takes twice as many blows but gets exactly the same wood/stone. Mixed actors, fractional progress, overkill and saves conserve yields without a second payout counter. Depletion remains permanent, with a `regrow` hook that schedules into the prop's own area.

**Catalog (data).** `content/build.ts` is the build catalog. `RECIPES` maps an id to `{name, category, desc, prop, v?, cost, requires, analysis?, learnFrom?, unknown?, place?, lit?, dur?, order?}`. `BUILD_CATEGORIES` gives the tab order. `requires` is a normal `Requirement` (`know.build`, `know.masonry`, ...). While it isn't met, an entry shows as locked (`unknown: "locked"`, the default, with "UNKNOWN RECIPE") or is left out of the menu (`"hidden"`), so a book or NPC can unlock recipes later just by setting a flag. Adding a buildable means adding a row here, its `PROPS` entry if the kind is new, and a `PROP_ART` sprite. The current rows (*scan*: analysis-gated, and what to scan for it; *free*: knowledge and materials only):
- LIGHT: torch post (raw: 2 wood + 1 stone; floor; free), wall torch (1 cut stone + 1 wood; wall; scan a wall torch), brazier (4 cut stone + 2 wood; floor; scan a brazier, and it needs `know.masonry`, which has no book yet, so it shows locked)
- CRAFT: crafting bench (raw: 6 wood + 2 stone; scan the artificery's workbench)
- DEFENSE: barricade (raw: 3 wood; blocks movement; free)
- FURNITURE: table (4 planks; scan a table), chair (2 planks; scan a chair), storage chest (4 planks; one tile, 32 slots; scan the treasury's old chests)
- DECOR: wall banner (1 plank + 1 cut stone; the authored `banner` prop, intact variant `v: 3`; scan a banner)

**Materials.** Rough builds stay cheap and raw; finer ones use crafted materials (planks, cut stone), replacing the raw ones or mixing both. The crafting bench must stay raw: planks come from a bench, so a bench that needed them could never be built (and a taken-apart one could never be rebuilt: its refund is its cost). `costTotal` (the menu's cost sort) weighs a crafted unit by what it costs to make (`rawValue`, derived from the craft rows), and search finds buildables by material name.

Most of these reuse existing prop kinds and art. The crafting bench builds the dungeon's own `workbench` prop (same sprite, same station), so the new kinds are `barricade` and `storage_chest` (which borrows the chest's sprite through `PropKindDef.art`).

**Catalog queries.** `sim/catalog.ts` holds pure functions. `catalogEntries` gives per-entry status (`ok` / `cost` / `unknown`) and cost-versus-carried lines. `matchesSearch` checks that every query word appears in the name, the category or a material name. `sortEntries` sorts by type, name or cost, and the order is total. `queryCatalog` combines tab, favorites, search, the can-build filter and the sort. `entryReason` produces the "why not" text. Tests drive these with synthetic catalogs of hundreds of entries.

**Placement rules.** `sim/build.ts` holds the rules. `placement()` = knowledge + cost (`canBuild`), reach (`BUILD_REACH` tiles, Chebyshev), then `siteFor()` by `place` rule:
- `floor`: the anchor tile and every tile under the prop's collision box must be open floor (`footprint()`; the bench and table span three tiles). No prop may be anchored in the footprint or block it. It may not overlap the rune or circle rings or any actor body.
- `wall`: the dungeon builder already anchors its wall torches, banners and chains this way, so "on a wall" means the **visible south face of a plain `WALL` tile with open floor directly below**. The prop anchors at the bottom edge of the wall tile (`tx*16+8, ty*16+16`), so it sorts like the authored ones (`wallMounted`). Side and back faces aren't drawn in this 3/4 view, so they never take props. Doors, cracked walls, pillars, rubble and thicket don't count as walls, so tutorial walls can never be covered. One wall-mounted prop is allowed per face (an authored torch or banner occupies its face). The surface has no masonry walls, so wall torches are dungeon-only for now; the torch post is the outdoor light.

`suggestTile()` starts the ghost on the front tile if it fits, else on the nearest fitting tile in reach (a wall torch snaps to the closest face). Building is the `build` effect: it re-validates, then calls `Area.addProp` (ids `kind#b<n>`, and the prop records its `recipe`). Built props' lights are reconciled by `AreaView` from `area.revision`.

**Deconstruction.** `orderDeconstruct` starts a `dismantle` task (`DECONSTRUCT_DUR`). Its `deconstruct` effect re-validates when it fires, because the prop may be gone, the actor out of reach, or something critical may have landed on it. It then calls `Area.removeProp`, spills anything non-critical resting on or in the prop to the ground (the contents rule for future containers), and refunds through `giveItem` for each cost row. A full pack drops the overflow on the ground like any pickup. Nothing is ever deleted. `salvageOf(prop)` decides the refund:
- *Built props* (`dyn`) refund what they cost when built (`prop.refund`, recorded by `applyBuild`; older saves: their recipe's cost, else the recipe for the kind).
- *Authored props* are deconstructable if their kind has a row in **`AUTHORED_SALVAGE`**, which is **derived from the catalog**: every prop kind a recipe builds is in it, refunding that recipe's full cost, crafted materials included. So the dungeon's wall torches, braziers, banners (every variant), tables, chairs and its workbench can all be taken apart, and a new buildable is deconstructable everywhere it appears with no extra row. `EXTRA_SALVAGE` is the data row for authored kinds no recipe builds (`{recipe}` or `{refund}`). A test audits both builders: every authored prop of a buildable kind refunds exactly its recipe.
- *Props that hold or gate progress refuse.* `deconstructCheck` returns `holds` while a progress-critical item (`isCritical`: the tutorial's undroppable items, and any book that teaches) still rests on or in the prop; the message names it ("TAKE THE RUNE CHISEL FIRST"), the effect re-checks when it fires, and nothing changes. Today that is the dungeon workbench (the rune chisel the carving needs) and the library table (the Builder's Primer, the only source of `know.build`); once the item is taken the prop can go, and the item can never be lost under the golem. The workbench is also the only crafting station until another is built, and its raw cost means taking it apart and rebuilding is always net zero. Torches, braziers, banners and chairs gate nothing (light only); the tutorial's locked door, cracked wall, rubble, pedestals and circle are not buildable kinds, so none can be touched.

Removing a prop bumps `revision`. Prop-owned area lights (`LightDef.owner`) and built-prop lights drop out of `AreaView.wantedLights`, and cached interactables re-derive. `deconTargetAt` picks the prop a remove cursor points at: one anchored in the tile, deconstructable ones first, else one whose box covers the tile.

**Build UI** (`screens/buildMenu.ts`). This is one `build` overlay with three modes:
- *menu*: tabs, a 6x5 scrolling grid of procedural sprite previews, a detail panel (preview, category, placement rule, description, cost vs carried, the reason it can't be built), search, sort, a can-build filter and favorites. Favorites are a presentation preference in `localStorage`, not world state.
- *place*: footprint or wall-face ghost, valid wall faces in reach marked, reach outline, status bar.
- *remove*: amber brackets on every deconstructable prop in view, the refund shown before confirming, a polite refusal otherwise.

Search uses a modal text field (`ui/kit.ts: TextField`): `/` or `T` enters typing mode, where letters go to the field and only arrows browse, so WASD and E never conflict. `Game.keyDown` collects raw printable characters into `Game.typed` each frame. The last used recipe is remembered (`B` opens on it, `V` re-places it). With repeat on (`T`), the play screen reopens the same tool once the ordered work finishes (`BuildUi.pending`). Rotation is deferred, since every sprite has a single orientation.

**Work stations and crafting.** `PropKindDef.station` (`workbench: "crafting"`) makes `deriveInteractables` emit a `station` interactable. `RULES.station` runs a short task whose `useStation` effect emits `openStation`, and the host pushes `screens/crafting.ts` (player only), so the sim has no special case. The crafting bench you build and the dungeon's authored workbench are the same `workbench` prop. While something rests on a station prop, picking that up is offered first (`propItem`), so the dungeon workbench still hands over its chisel (the tutorial needs that) and is a bench from then on. **Knowledge**: `STATION_REQUIRES` (`content/crafting.ts`) says what a crafter must know to read a station's plans (`know.build` for the crafting bench). Without it the station still opens, but the screen is greyed out: `craftEntries` returns nothing and CRAFT only reports `CRAFT_LOCKED_TEXT`. Every craft, rune carving included, uses this one existing knowledge: no crafting flag or book.

**Crafting** (`content/crafting.ts` rows, `sim/crafting.ts`). `CRAFT_RECIPES`: `{id, name, station, desc, inputs, tools?, output, requires?, dur?}`. Ratios and durations live only in these rows: planks (2 wood -> 3), cut stone (3 stone -> 2), Rune of Swift (2 cut stone) and Rune of Glow (3 cut stone), the runes with `tools: ["chisel"]`. `tools` must be carried but are never consumed. `craftEntry` gives a status (`ok`, `cost`, `tools`, `unknown`) and the lines the screen shows. `orderCraft(w, a, station, id, propId?)` (the CRAFT button) validates and starts a `craft` task aimed at the bench; the `craft` effect (`applyCraft`) re-validates when it fires (the bench may be gone, the inputs spent, the chisel dropped, the knowledge lost: a failure changes nothing and says why), then removes the inputs and gives the output with `giveItem`, so a full pack leaves the overflow on the ground like any pickup. Any actor can run the same task from its queue, and a craft in progress is saved like any task. CRAFT closes the screen so the golem can work with the world running (`CraftingUi.pending`); when the work is done the host reopens the plans with the cursor where it was.

### Off-screen simulation
**Policy: tick everything that is loaded.** Every area the world has instantiated is simulated every `stepWorld`: non-player actors' tasks, the ritual charge, and the global schedule. Areas are tiny state records, so this is cheap, needs no catch-up maths, and keeps determinism trivial. Two guards make work that spans a trip safe:
- `Scheduled` entries carry the `area` they were scheduled in and always apply there (a regrowing tree regrows on the surface while the golem is underground).
- Tasks record the `area` they were started in. `enterArea` drops queued work aimed at the area being left, and `tickTask` discards a task that would fire in another area.
If areas ever become expensive (many NPCs), switch unvisited areas to catch-up on entry using `world.time - area.lastTicked`. The guards above already make that switch local.

### Large areas
`AreaView` bakes the background in 8x8-tile chunks: those under the camera on demand, the rest one chunk per frame. Light visibility is traced the first time a light reaches the view window. Dirty-rect re-bakes and re-traces are unchanged. Measured in Node with the recording canvas (`npm test` prints it), for the 96x72 surface: a full bake took 420–600 ms. Chunked, the first frame takes about 40 ms (8 chunks, behind the arrival fade), then about 4 ms per frame in the background. Only lights near the view are traced (4 of 8 at the rune).

### Save size
A tile edit costs about 9 bytes in the diff (3000 surface edits: about 27 KB). That is fine for now. If terraforming ever touches tens of thousands of tiles, store a run-length or base64 grid per area once the diff exceeds a threshold.

### NPCs and dialogue
**Every NPC is an Actor** with a stable id (`npc.guardian`). Its `definition` selects the unified body/art/capability row; an optional `npc` field selects a dialogue identity in `content/npcs.ts` (`NpcDef`: name, type, script and actor-definition id). The legacy `kind: "npc"` is retained as metadata, not used to determine capacity, jobs or collision. Nothing in the sim, interaction path or renderer branches on a particular NPC:
- *Placement*: an area builder lists `npcs` (spawn rows). `getArea` turns them into actors on first instantiation. After that they are saved like any actor. The guardian sits against the treasury's north wall between the looted chests. Its box keeps the doorway and rows 5 to 8 clear, which `sim/reach.ts` checks in the tests. The dormant worker golem is a second spawn row, in the artificery, with no NPCS row: `makeNpc` falls back to its `npc` string as the actor definition.
- *Collision*: `actorBox` (from the unified ActorDef body) feeds `boxFree`/`nearestFree`/`dropSpot`; the player and workers block each other too. Movers and rune arrivals use their own footprint rather than a fixed player-sized box.
- *Interaction*: `npcInteractables(w, area)` derives one interactable per actor standing in the area (actors live on the World, not the Area, so these are not in the area's revision cache): `"worker"` for an actor with the `worker` capability (`RULES.worker` -> `manageActor`), else `"npc"` for one with an NpcDef. The talk target carries `actorId` and `npc`. `RULES.npc` starts a short `talk` task whose `talk` effect opens the NPC's dialogue. Every talk first emits `talkedTo` `{actor, npc}`, whether a segment follows (`dialogue`) or only the idle line (a plain `message`), so something that must react to every interaction listens to that. The task is area-bound like every other task.
- *Rendering*: `view/actors.ts` looks up the ActorDef's art key, drawing albedo and emissive passes. The guardian still uses `NPC_ART` and the host's eased `NpcLook`; workers derive their minimal pose from their task, job and power. The guardian's eye glow remains a dynamic AreaView light. No golem or guardian golden hash changed.
- *State*: an NPC's runtime state is a flag under its id (`npc.<id>.awake`; absent = dormant), set only through the `npcAwake` effect. I chose a flag over an actor field because dialogue requirements already read flags, saves need no actor schema change, and "dormant" stays a plain reversible switch: repair or speech later only sets it again.
- *Special vs generic*: `NpcType` is `"special"` only. Generic NPCs (archetypes, stats, randomized looks, personality) will add `"generic"` and their own fields. None of that exists yet.

**Dialogue** (`content/dialogue.ts`, `sim/dialogue.ts`): scripts are data keyed by script id. A script is ordered **segments** (`when`: a `Requirement`, `done`: completion flag, `pages`). Talking plays *the first segment whose requirement is met and whose done flag is unset*, so progress is derived from flags and there is no saved cursor. A page has lines, an optional speaker, optional `when` (flavour pages, e.g. the guardian noticing the iron arm), and optional **beats**: `beat` fires when the page shows, `then` when it is dismissed. Beats are a registry (`BEATS`) of named handlers that return plain Effects (`npcAwake`, `repairEye`, `npcGive` with a `once` guard flag, `segmentDone`). Every effect is idempotent, so replaying or resuming never repeats a gift. The commands `talkTo`, `dialogueBeat` (validated: the segment must be playable and contain the beat) and `finishSegment` are what the screen calls. Tests call them too, through `playSegment`.

The **dialogue screen** is a non-opaque overlay: the world pauses beneath it while presentation (particles, NPC pose, zoom) keeps animating. It shows a bottom box with the speaker tag and a typewriter reveal with a per-character blip. `E`/`Enter`/`Space` completes the page, then advances. `Esc` leaves, and the next talk resumes at the first unfinished segment. Beat timing (holds) is presentation-only (`BEAT_HOLD`). Sound and particles come from the `dialogue`, `npcState` and `eyeRepaired` events.

**Dialogue text** is authored as one plain paragraph per page (`DialoguePage.text`; `\n` forces a break). `ui/dialogueLayout.ts` word-wraps it (`ui/textWrap.ts`: greedy, words longer than a line are broken) to the box's real text width and paginates it to the box's line capacity, both derived from the box geometry: 292 px box, 10 px inset each side, 4 px per character including the shadow = **68 characters**; lines at a 10 px pitch must clear the footer row = **4 lines**. One authored page can become several screens. Its `beat` fires on the first and its `then` on the last, so beats still land on the right page. The typewriter, blips, speaker tag and skip/advance run on the wrapped screens unchanged. Author page breaks where the speaker pauses, not to fit the box. Tests lint the wrapped output (font glyphs only, nothing wider than the box, at most 4 lines), cover the wrapper and pagination (long words, exact fits), and render every guardian screen through `tests/support/uiSnapshot.ts` to check that no text pixel leaves the box and that long pages reach the right margin.

**The Treasury Guardian** (first special NPC): segments `intro` (wake beat), `repair` (`repairEye`: `golem.eye`, crackle-to-chime sfx, sparks, eye flare, the guardian's hand holds still, the camera eases out), `core` (`giveCore` via `giveItem`, so a full pack drops the core on the ground), and `farewell` (`goDormant` on the last page). Afterwards talking shows its idle line. The treasury's `chest@57,4` is built open and empty (`PropDef.open`). The core exists only with the guardian, who stands in the always-reachable treasury before the core matters. In play, the eye no longer gutters once `golem.eye` is set (the random sputter now drives only the core, since the body is still damaged), and the golem art's eye flicker stops.

**Planned (not built)**: repair knowledge and speech will make the guardian repairable and recruitable. Each is a new segment row gated on new flags, with a `wake` beat, plus new effects for the repair work itself. Generic NPCs reuse the actor, interaction and render paths with their own data source.

## Deliberately deferred
- **Fixed-timestep simulation.** It would make the sim independent of frame rate, but on high-refresh displays it changes motion feel unless rendering interpolates. That's a visible change, so the host keeps variable `dt`. `stepWorld/stepPlayer` already take `dt`, so the switch only touches the host.
- **Entity/component framework.** `Actor` remains a plain record with optional dialogue identity and durable order, selected by a data definition and capabilities. No ECS or inheritance hierarchy is needed for these foundations.
- **Job board / automatic allocation.** Durable explicit orders and pathfinding exist; priorities, assignment UI and autonomous selection of orders wait for content.
- **A job board, recruitment and the final vision renderer.** The worker and storage screens exist (see "Worker management and storage screens"); orders are still one per worker, given by hand. A board of open jobs, automatic allocation and recruitment are later.
- **Archetype, stats and personality data for generic NPCs.** Dialogue exists (special NPCs). Generic-NPC data waits for the generic-NPC work.
- **Recruiting and following.** Jobs and pathfinding exist; recruitment story conditions and follow behaviour are later handlers/content.
- **Save slots, autosave, a save menu.** Only the single K/L slot exists.

## Dormant worker lock and chest names

- **The dormant lock.** A body that needs power (`ActorDef.power`) only opens its management screen once a core has been in it: `canManage` (sim/power.ts) is true with a core in, or once the world flag `<actorId>.coreSeated` (`CORE_SEATED`) is set. `insertPowerCore` and `removePowerCore` both set it, so taking the core out again keeps the screen available. - **Chest names.** `PropDef.name` is an optional player-given name, saved as part of the prop's state (`PropState.name`, or in `propsAdded` for built props), with no version bump. `propName(area, prop)` (sim/orders.ts) is the one name: the player's, else `defaultPropName` (area, kind, and a number once the area has several of that kind: DUNGEON CHEST 1, DUNGEON CHEST 2), which is what the order lists always used. ## The market town (Westwake)

West of the surface, where the Treasury Guardian said a village was. One outdoor area (`town`, 96 x 68) plus six interior areas (`town_smithy`, `town_armory`, `town_jeweler`, `town_tailor`, `town_tavern`, `town_guild`). Appearance only for now: no NPCs, no shop systems, no day/night. Everything below is used by the town or its tests.

**The edge and the wall.** The town has no `THICKET` border: every tile that is not wall or building is ground, right up to the last row and column, and the map simply ends there. Nothing else is needed to hold the player in: `boxFree` treats out of bounds as solid and the camera is clamped to the area, so the golem and the view both stop at the last tile. (The bake does not shade ground at the map's edge as if a wall stood beyond it; scatter keeps solid and tall props off the outermost ring so no sprite is cut by the edge.) The wall (`content/town.ts: STREETS.wall`) is a closed ring of `WALL` tiles styled `WS.RAMPART`, two tiles thick, with a third row on the north and south runs for the visible south face. It is open only on the High Street's rows, where it passes between a pair of `tower` buildings at each end: the east gate (the `east_road` portal beyond it) and the West Bar, where the `tollbar` prop spans the passage exactly (its box is the width of the street), so the only way through the wall is the east gate. `art/tilesTown.ts: rampartTopPix` takes a bitmask of the eight neighbours that drop away from a wall-walk tile (open ground, or the wall's own south face) and draws a parapet on each such side plus a corner post on the inside corners of the ring. Building decals are moved to the end of the town's decal list, so houses are painted over the shade, moss, straw and wear around them rather than under them. `world/save.ts: settle` (run by `loadFromStorage`; `deserialize` stays the exact inverse of `serialize`) sets the player down on the nearest free ground if a save made in an older layout would load them inside a solid tile.

**Layout audit.** `tests/support/townAudit.ts` (`node scripts/townAudit.mjs`) reads the built town and interiors the way the game does (tiles, collision boxes, sprites, light positions, reachability from the area's start) and reports hand-placement mistakes: boxes in walls or off the map, overlapping solid props, wall-mounted props on no wall, lights inside opaque tiles, blocked or unreachable `Spot`s, sealed-off ground, blocked or covered doors, decals over roofs, and building rows that contradict themselves. `tests/townWall.test.ts` asserts it finds nothing, alongside the tests for the ring, the bar, the map's edge and the save guard. Placement note that the audit exists to catch: `Pt`/`Q` take a tile index and centre the prop on that tile (tile 47 is centred at 47.5 tiles), while spots and decals take raw tile coordinates; mirroring a layout about a door or a room's axis has to use the right one.

**Where things live.**
- `content/town.ts`: the data all three layers agree on. `BUILDINGS` (one row per building: footprint, wall and roof material, door, windows, how it is lit, sign, interior), `STREETS`, `SIGNS`, `ESTABLISHMENTS` / `HIGH_END`, and the shared geometry helpers (`doorPx`, `chimneyPx`, `dormerPx`). The file's header is the town's story and how to read it.
- `town.ts`: the builder. Lays ground and streets, stamps `BUILDINGS`, then dresses the place by hand (lamps, market, yards, fences, tracks) and scatters ground cover with its own seeded rng. `areaKit()` is the small prop / decal / light helper it shares with the interiors.
- `townInteriors.ts`: `ROOMS`, one hand-placed layout per shop, in room coordinates.
- `art/tilesTown.ts`, `art/town.ts`, `art/propsTown.ts`: pixels. `art/tiles.ts` and `art/decals.ts` only delegate to them for styles and kinds they do not know, so no existing pixel moved (the two legacy goldens in `tests/artRegistry.test.ts` still pass unchanged: the dungeon and surface sprites, and the surface baked without its new road).
- `sim/portals.ts`: walking between areas.

**Portals (walk-through connections).** Rune travel is a channelled jump; a town needs doors. `AreaData.portals` rows (`map.ts: Portal`) are a box of ground plus the direction you push to go through, declared in pairs. `stepPlayer` asks `portalAt` after moving; `walkThrough` moves the actor with `enterArea` to the paired portal's arrival spot (nudged to free ground), turns it to face away from the way in, and emits `portal`. The host answers that event with a latch or footfall, a short fade from dark (`doorFade`) and the arrival portal's `label` as a note. Arrivals never re-trigger: you arrive facing away from the portal, and it only fires while you push into it. Nothing here is player-specific: an NPC that paths to a portal box calls the same `walkThrough`. The surface's end of the road is cut in `buildSurface` after everything else and without the rng, so no existing prop id or pixel changes (`buildSurface({ westRoad: false })` is the old surface, bit for bit); the edge column stays thicket, as the wild's tests require.

**Buildings.** Seen like the dungeon's walls: from the south and above, so every front faces south and every door opens onto the street on its south side. A building is its footprint in solid tiles under one baked `building` decal (`art/town.ts: paintBuilding`, drawn once per building and cached). The bottom row of the footprint is `T.FRONT`: solid, but not opaque, so a lamp in the street lights both storeys of the front while the wall mass behind still stops the light (roofs stay dark; light never leaks through a house). Anything that glows or stands proud is a prop laid over the painting by the builder: lit windows (`winglow*`, positioned from the same constants the painter uses), signs, door lamps, chimney pots (the `smoke` row on a prop kind makes the host trail smoke).

**Interiors.** One area per shop, so "which shop am I in" is the area id. Each room is centred in a 44 x 26 area of solid wall (at least the 2x zoom's view, so the camera never leaves the area). `AreaData.interior` marks a lived-in room: no cave rumble, no falling grit, no leaves or fireflies. `WS.*` wall styles (the `style` of a WALL tile, 20 and up) give each house its own wall face.

**Light as class.** Two kinds of light, never mixed by accident: light runes (`rune_lamp`, `rune_sconce`, `runestand`, `winglow_rune`: blue-white, `flick: 0`) and fire (the dungeon's `torch`, the surface's `torch_post`, `lantern`, `candlestand`, hearths: orange, flickering). `Building.light` and `HIGH_END` decide which a house gets; `tests/town.test.ts` checks the split (no rune lamp south of the square, no torch on the High Street, high-end rooms burn nothing). There is no day/night system; the town uses the surface's dusk ambient so the contrast is always on show.

**Laid out for later.** `AreaData.spots` (`map.ts: Spot`) names the standing places the next systems need: `keeper` behind every counter and `customer` in front of it, a `seat` at every stool, a `stall` pitch behind every market stall, a `door` step at every house, `idle` corners (the Charter Stone, the well, the gate watch), `work` places (anvil, hoist, the golem stands). Nothing reads them yet; the tests prove each is free and can be walked to. Streets are two or more tiles wide, counters run east-west with a way round one end, and the line from each door to its counter is clear. Adding shopkeepers is: an `npcs` row per shop at its `keeper` spot, plus a dialogue script. Adding a shop screen is: a `station` on the counter kind.

**Labour golems.** The lore has only plain hauling golems here, and none are placed. Their absence is authored instead: `golempad` stands and the `bays` building in the stone yard, `yokerack`, `tallyboard`, `crane`, `stonestack` (lifting rings), `hauler` doors (`DOOR.hauler`), `golemtrack` decals along the haul road, the smithy's `bellows`, the tavern's `cellarhatch`. Most carry an `examine` line.

**Adding to the town.** A house: one `BUILDINGS` row (the builder stamps, paints, lights and gives it a doorstep). A seventh shop: add it to `ESTABLISHMENTS`, give its building an `interior`, add a `ROOMS` layout and an `AREA_NAMES` row; the registry, portals and tests pick it up. A new prop: a `TOWN_PROPS` row and a `TOWN_PROP_ART` entry (keep sprites within 96 x 60 px, the renderer's culling margin).

**Deferred.** NPCs and their pathing over `spots`; shop screens; a day/night cycle (the rune / fire split is built for it: runes never go out, fires would); a second rune site in the town; interiors for ordinary houses.

### Day and night (v13)

`World.day` is the calendar clock: days since the world began, the fraction the time of day. `sim/daytime.ts` is its only writer: `stepDay` (called from `stepWorld`, so the clock stops when the world does), `advanceDay`, `advanceDayTo` (the next time it is that hour) and `advanceDayUpTo` (only if that hour is still ahead today: safe to call every frame with a rising target, which is how a time-lapse is done). The clock only runs forward. Crossing into another part of the day emits one `dayPhase` event.

**Everything else is derived; nothing is stored.** `content/daycycle.ts` is pure maths over the hour: `sunLevel`, `ambientAt(night, hour, exposure)`, `lampGain`, `skyLight`, `litLevel(habit, id, hour)`. A save holds the clock and no lamp states. The town cannot get out of step with the hour, the v12 -> v13 migration had one number to add, and a watched and a skipped cutscene cannot leave different worlds.

- *An area's `ambient` is its night.* The authored look is untouched at night; the sun is added on top by how much sky the area sees (`exposureOf`: `outdoor`, `interior`, or neither, which is buried and never changes). `Lighting.compose` takes a `gain` that scales lights against the ambient, so a lamp at noon adds little and art authored for the dark is not overexposed.
- *Tended lights.* `PropKindDef.habit` ("rune" / "fire" / "window") says how a kind of light is kept; `AreaData.tended` says people live here and keep them. Both must hold: the same `torch` kind burns on untended in the halls, and props the golem built (`dyn`) are never tended. The host asks `litLevel` per prop id (a stable hash gives each lamp its own minute) for three things that have to agree: the light's strength (`Light.day`; `AreaView` tags the light with its owner prop's habit when it creates it), the sprite (the unlit art for `alt: "lit"` kinds, the emissive layer faded for lamps, the whole overlay faded for a window) and the fire visuals (flame, embers). The market cross has no habit on purpose: its inscription says its rune has never gone out.
- *Sky light.* A `LightDef` with `sky` is the sky reaching in (the throne room's roof crack, a room's doorway): it keeps its authored colour by night and takes the sun's by day.

The first crossing sets the hour in the sim (`sim/runes.ts: travel`, on the player's first jump: forward to 04:00), not in the cutscene, so headless tests and skipped cutscenes get it too.

### The crossing (the first rune jump)

`screens/crossing.ts` replaces the old end card (a painted scene that matched nothing in the world) and the separate arrive screen with one timeline, `content/cutscenes.ts: CROSSING`, in the same beat format as the opening (`ui/cutscene.ts` takes any `BeatScript`). Beat labels mark the acts. The transit is a card (`screens/cards.ts: renderTransit`, pure drawing from the screen's clock). The arrival is the host's existing rune awakening, now `arriveStep(t, dt)` on `ARRIVE`'s timings (data, beside the script). First light advances the real calendar clock with `advanceDayUpTo`, so the dawn in the cutscene is the morning the player is handed. The host's `tele` screen is still the lift-off; it hands over once the sim has travelled.

**Skipping is finishing.** Each cutscene ends through one function shared by its control beat and by Esc. A skip first thinks every remaining line through the sim (`playerThinks`, so `monologue.said` is the same either way) and clears the log so they are not shown. If the jump itself has not happened yet, `Game.skipCrossing` lets the sim finish the channel task first. `tests/daycycle.test.ts` skips at every stage and holds the world to the watched one.

Where the golem looks during the cutscene is `Game.gaze`, a presentation override. The sim's `facing` is not touched and the player is never stepped, as in the opening.

## The dungeon audit

The tutorial dungeon was audited by rendering all of it and looking: `tests/support/dungeonSnapshot.ts` (run by `scripts/dungeonPreview.mjs`) drives the real host on a pixel canvas, zooms the view out past the area's size so the whole map is one frame, and draws it `lit` (the game's own lighting), `dim` (the same lights over a raised ambient) and `flat` (albedo and glow only). Because it is the host that draws, the frame has what an area-only render misses: the tile sprites (rubble, the cracked wall, pillars, the door, bars), the NPCs, flames and the roof crack's shaft. The town's layout audit (`tests/support/townAudit.ts: auditArea`) is run over the dungeon as well.

What was wrong, and what was done:

- **The cracked wall did not look like wall.** It was two free-standing slabs, lighter than any masonry near them, drawn over a floor tile, with the wall above still showing its south face over them. A cracked wall is wall until it is broken: `T.CRACKED` is wall mass now (`content/tiles.ts: isWallMassT`), so the bake draws it as the top of the wall it stands in (the same dark top and rim, exactly one tile thick, flush with the masonry above and below, the floor beside it shaded as beside any wall, no face on the wall above it). Its tile sprite (`art/tiles.ts: rubbleSprite(_, true)`) redraws that same top and adds only the damage that marks it as breakable: sprung joints, capstones heaved out of line past the rim, bites out of the rim, fallen stone along its foot on both sides, and one fissure from end to end with the light of the hall behind it (it enters and leaves each tile at the same column, so a run of cracked tiles carries an unbroken crack). Collision, light and the interaction are unchanged; breaking it turns the tiles to floor and the re-bake (which already pads two tiles) gives the wall above its face back.
- **The roof crack's shaft ended on a hard edge.** The sprite was a wedge cut off flat at its top. It is taller now and has no top: over its upper half it thins out pixel by pixel until nothing is left (`game.ts: buildShaft`). The foot and the pool on the floor are where they were.
- **Braziers looked as if they lay on the floor.** Their legs were one pixel wide in the darkest iron and vanished on dark marble and cobbles. The brazier is a tripod now: each leg a bright core inside a dark outline (one of the two always shows), splayed to feet wider than the bowl, braced, with the bowl's shadow on the floor between them. Variant 1 used to differ only by a transparent rectangle (a no-op); it is an old one with spilled ash. The town's braziers are the same sprite and gain the same legs.
- **Some barracks beds looked like two beds with a hole between them.** The "broken" cot was drawn with a black band across its middle, the same black as the missing planks of the floor. The unmade cot keeps its frame whole and shows its wear inside it (the blanket slid off, a stained and split straw mattress, a cracked rail and footboard). The south row lay head-first towards its own footlockers; `bed` has a second variant bit (head to the south), used by that row, so every footlocker stands at the foot of its bed.
- **The throne hall's pillars stood on each other.** A pillar is drawn three tiles tall; the hall had one every third row, so each base sat on the capital in front of it. They stand every fourth row now, as in the great hall (three a side, one broken on each side as before). The procedural scatter draws from one rng in reading order, so it still walks those ten tiles as they lay before: no crack, bone or cobweb anywhere in the dungeon moved.
- **The chapel had three axes.** The altar stood a tile and a half east of its pews, against the side door, and the banner a tile and a half west of them. The banner, the altar and its candle light are on the pews' axis now. Both props keep their ids (`altar@26,48`, `banner@23,46`), which saves and tests know them by.
- **Four great hall statues stood in the side walls** (their plinths' collision boxes, by a pixel). They stand two pixels clear, with the ids they had.
- **Roots ran across the top of a wall.** Three of the SURFACE GATE's root decals trailed over the Vestibule's south wall. They start higher and stay on the floor and the gate's rubble.

Left alone on purpose (the audit reports them; `tests/dungeonAudit.test.ts` names them): the boulders heaped on the gate's rubble and the seam of night air buried in it, and the four places nobody can walk to yet (the throne hall behind the cracked wall, the treasury behind its locked door, two gaol cells behind their bars).

The pixel goldens that moved (`tests/art.test.ts`, `tests/artRegistry.test.ts`) were checked against the art as it was, pixel for pixel: of the prop sprites only `bed` and `brazier` differ, of the tile sprites only the cracked wall, and of the dungeon's bake only the cracked wall's tiles with the ring round them and the tiles under the three moved roots.

## The entrance update (v14)

Tools, the smelter and the anvil, iron ore, digging, and the stair between the surface and the halls. It is data rows plus a few small pure-sim functions; `tests/smithing.test.ts` and `tests/entrance.test.ts` hold it.

**Where the halls are.** The dungeon is the first cellar floor. What stood above it is gone under the grass, and only what was warded survives up there: the rune circle, and a glyph cut in the lintel of the stair that came up into that floor. So the way in is a staircase, not a gate at ground level. In the halls it is a flight climbing south out of the Vestibule (`content/geography.ts: GATE_STAIR`: the old gate corridor plus the exit row); on the surface it is the stair head lying over the flight's top rows (the `buriedgate` prop, id unchanged, now centred on the stair).

**Stations are data.** `content/crafting.ts: STATIONS` has three rows: `crafting` (the bench), `smelting`, `smithing`. A row gives the title, the prop kind whose sprite heads the screen, the action's word, what a crafter must know (`requires`; `STATION_REQUIRES` is derived from it), what the station says while that is unknown, an accent, and optional tabs. `PropKindDef.station` names one: the artificery's `forge` (the smelter; the kind keeps its id, so its prop id and the worker's alcove note still hold) is `smelting`, `anvil` is `smithing`. Nothing else was needed for E to work on them: `deriveInteractables` already emits a `station` interactable for any prop with one, and `RULES.station` / `useStation` / `openStation` are unchanged. One screen (`screens/crafting.ts`) serves all three; the host opens it for any `isStation` id. `CraftRecipe` gained four optional fields: `tab` (which tab lists it), `verb` (the button's word for this row), `work` (the piece the row works on: such a row is listed only while that piece is at hand, `sim/crafting.ts: craftList`, so the anvil's repair, modify and rune tabs show what can be done to what was brought, not every variant) and `also` (a second product: the rune pried back out of a head). Worker orders bind any station prop and list that station's rows, so a worker can be told to smelt with no new code.
- *The smelter*: IRON INGOT (2 ore + 1 wood -> 2) and INGOTS, FULL CHARGE (6 ore + 2 wood -> 6; `valuation: false`, so an ingot is always valued by the first).
- *The anvil*, four tabs: FORGE (ingots into a shovel, pick or axe head: 2 / 3 / 3), REPAIR (a rusted head + 1 ingot into a sound one; never the head's valuation row), MODIFY (temper a head, or a hafted tool, for 2 ingots), RUNES (set a Rune of Swift or Glow into a tempered head; UNSET gives both back).
- *The bench*: a head + 2 wood is the hafted tool (the fine row waits for a fine head).
- *Knowledge.* The bench and the anvil read the existing `know.build`. Smelting is its own flag, `know.smelt`, and no book grants it: `Recipe.teaches` on the SMELTER build row makes `learnPlans` grant it when the plan is read, by a scan or by taking the smelter apart. Until then the smelter opens greyed out, like the bench before the Primer.
- *Build rows*: SMELTER (6 cut stone + 4 stone, builds `forge`; no iron, since iron comes out of it) and ANVIL (5 ingots + 2 wood), both analysis-gated on their own kind. `AUTHORED_SALVAGE` is derived, so the artificery's own can be taken apart for exactly that; as with the bench, rebuilding is net zero.

**Tools (`content/tools.ts`, `sim/tools.ts`).** Three types (shovel, pickaxe, axe), two forms. A *head* is gear of kind `arm`: fitted, it adds its `bonus` to the work multiplier of every blow against resources of its type (`HarvestDef.tool`: axe for trees and logs, pickaxe for rocks and iron veins; `harvestStep` multiplies), and the shovel and pickaxe let the golem dig. A *full tool* (head + haft) is for hands: it counts in the pack of a body with the `wield` capability (both worker bodies), and does nothing for the maintainer. `toolBonus(a, type)` is the one reader; it takes only the actor, so `meets` can use it (`Requirement.tools`) without an import cycle. Two of a kind do not add.
- *Grades and insets are item rows, not item state.* Per type the head exists plain (bonus 1), rusted (found in the ruins, no use until repaired), fine (tempered, bonus 2) and fine with either rune set in it (the rune's `worn` effect, minus its sigil, which is drawn for rune slots only); the hafted tool plain and fine. That is 21 rows, generated from the table in `content/items.ts`, with icons generated the same way. The reason: a pack slot is `{item, n}`, gear is `slot -> item`, and loose stacks, chests, transfers, crafting inputs and saves all move items by id. Per-item state would have to be carried through every one of those paths; rows cost nothing there, and every existing rule (wear, take off, store, drop, save) applies as it is.
- *What that gives up:* tools do not wear out in use, since wear is exactly per-item state. REPAIR therefore works on what time ruined: two rusted heads are authored (a shovel head by the smelter's coal bin, an axe head in the armory), each a cheaper first tool than forging. Wear in use is the first thing a per-item state layer would add; the REPAIR tab and rows are where it would land.
- *Not drawn yet:* a head fitted to an arm does not show on the golem's sprite (`GolemPose` has no arm fitting), only in the pack's paper doll.

**Iron ore.** `HARVEST.ironore`: 6 work, half a piece per unit, so two bare-handed blows a piece and three pieces a vein; no regrowth, like everything else. `buildSurface` places `IRON_VEINS` (12) last and by hash, rocky ground first, through the builder's own `canPlace` (so each keeps the free ring that prevents pockets) and never through the rng: no other prop or decal moved (tested against `buildSurface({ iron: false })`). It is harvested by the slam like any rock, and the worker's GATHER form offers it with no new code.

**Digging (`content/digging.ts`, `sim/digging.ts`).** The ground is a column of layers; a layer is a material and an amount of work; a material names its tool, its yield and its `RULES` row. E on a dig site picks the row for the material of the layer reached (`digDirt` / `digStone`): with the tool, a `punch` task whose `dig` effect re-validates when it lands and clears `bonus` units (never running on into the next layer, which needs its own tool), paying one unit of the material per unit cleared and emitting `dug` (with `next` when a layer gives out, `opened` when the column is through); without it, a message that says which head it needs. Progress is the prop's `dmg` (saved already, as for harvesting); `content/digging.ts: digStage` turns it into the sprite variant (`propLook`, used by the renderer and by golem vision).
- *Only the entrance today, anywhere later.* `DIG_SITES` maps a prop kind to a column, and its only row is the buried stair head (8 earth, 8 stone). `sim/digging.ts: digSpot` is the one place that knows a site is a prop; everything after it works on a column and a depth. Digging any surface tile is two additions: a per-tile depth store on `Area` (a save field with a migration), and a default column per ground style (`GROUND_COLUMN` is the one the meadow would get), returned by the same lookup for a tile that has no site. The rules, the tool check, the yields, the events and the host's feedback would not change.

**The entrance (`content/geography.ts: ENTRANCES`, `sim/digging.ts: openEntrance`).** An entrance is a way between two areas that has to be opened first. Its row names a world flag, the portal at each end, and what the fall filled at the lower end. A dig site with `opens` calls `openEntrance` when its column is through: the flag is set, the lower end's tiles become floor (the stair's steps were under the rubble all along, as the floor style `FS.STAIR`), the rock piles and the fall standing on them are removed, and the prop that owns the stair's daylight is lit. `Portal.flag` shuts a portal until its flag is set (`sim/portals.ts: portalOpen`, checked by `portalAt`'s filter and again by `walkThrough`, which is not player-specific: whoever finds the way in later uses the same function). `PropKindDef.openBox` is a footprint that only blocks once the prop is open: the buried stair head is flat, walkable ground; dug out, it is a hole, entered only from its south lip, where the portal's box lies. Anyone standing on it when it opens is set down on that lip. All of it is existing save fields (prop `dmg` / `open` / `lit`, a flag, tile diffs, `propsRemoved`), which is why v14 adds none. `AreaData.spots` gained `stairhead` (surface) and `stairfoot` (dungeon) for the NPCs that will come looking.

**How the two ends read.** *Below*, it used to read as a gate at ground level with night air coming through. ## Rocks and the stair, redrawn

Art only. Nothing here is simulated or saved: no prop moved or changed size, footprint, variant or id, no tile or style changed, both builders give what they gave. So **there is no schema change and no migration** (`SAVE_VERSION` stays 14; `tests/rockArt.test.ts` pins the sizes, boxes and placements that claim rests on). It was done the way the dungeon audit was: render it, look, fix, render again (`scripts/rockPreview.mjs`).

What was wrong:

- **Every rock was the same two ellipses.** `rock`, `ironore`, `boulder` and `outcrop` were each `stoneBlob` twice (three times for the outcrop): one oval with a smaller oval on its shoulder, ringed in outline. A round stone is right for a heap of them; for a single standing rock that is to be read as a thing to break, it is not a rock. The vein was that same shape with a dozen orange pixels on it.
- **The stair was three different stairs, and none looked like one.** The head on the surface was thin grey slats parted by black lines; the flight in the halls was flat eight-pixel bands ruled off by a hairline (boards, under a warm light), with a pale stripe down every tile instead of down the flight; the foot was three lighter slabs of its own. Three pitches, three palettes.
- **The fall was a wall.** Dug down to stone, the stair head showed coursed, mortared blocks, and broken through, the same courses with some missing. From below the same fall is a heap of boulders.

### Standing rock (`art/rocks.ts`)

A rock is a few convex **chunks**. A chunk is what is left of a block when planes are cut off it: steep faces round an elliptical footprint, gentler ones across the shoulders, a tilted top (`chunk(rng, cx, cy, rx, ry, h, opt)`; the solid is where `n . P <= d` for every plane, above the ground). `paintRock` draws chunks as the rest of the game is drawn, from the south and above with a unit of height a pixel up the screen: each pixel is a ray `P(t) = (x, y + t, t)`, the last point of it inside a chunk is what shows (for a convex solid that is one min and one max over the planes), and the plane it lands on is the facet. The facet's angle to one light (upper left, a little toward the viewer, as every sprite is lit) picks one of four tones of the existing stone ramp. Then: an outline round the silhouette, a dark seam where one chunk stands in front of another, a brighter row where a face ends above a darker one (the ridge), the foot a tone down, a few pits. No new palette for plain rock: it is the halls' `S` ramp plus one ridge glint.

What is cut into the stone is placed on the solid, not on the sprite, because each pixel knows its 3D point: a **crack** runs down a lit face; **moss** lies in cushions on what faces the sky and leaves steep faces bare; an **ore seam** is a slightly warped sheet through the rock, so it crosses a face, turns at the ridge and comes out the other side, shaded with the face it crosses, with rust washed down the stone beneath it. Ore is the iron ore icon's colours; its host is `IRONSTONE`, the same ramp darker and warmer, so a vein reads as one at a glance and the ore shows against it.

**Shapes are data.** `rockForm(kind, seed)` builds the chunks from a seed and nothing else (not the sprite's rng), and `ROCK_SEEDS` is the table of seeds per kind and variant. They were picked by eye: `node scripts/rockPreview.mjs pick` renders sixty candidates a kind with their seed numbers; reshaping a variant is changing one number. Kinds differ in build, not only size: a `rock` is one block with clean faces and usually a piece split from it; an `ironore` vein is a tall broken tooth, a shelf against it and a fallen piece; a `boulder` is broad and many-faced with its shoulders worn off, under moss; an `outcrop` is one tilted mass with ledges either side. So the harvestable (bare, sharp, cracked) tells from the scenery (worn, mossed) without a marker. The broken state (`taken`) is a scatter of angular chips, flecked with ore for a vein.

**Loose stone stays round.** `stoneBlob` is untouched and is still what rubble, the dungeon's `rockpile`, `stairfall` and the `cairn` are made of. That is the line: stone that stands is cut, stone that fell or was stacked is rounded.

### One stair (`art/stairs.ts`)

The flight climbs south and is seen from the south and above, so no riser can show (drawing risers would make it a stair going down to the south, away from the surface it leads to). What makes treads alone read as a stair is in one place, and all three drawings take it from there:

- `stepShade(row, pitch)`: a step is shaded as a solid, not ruled off: a lit lip at its north edge, then the tread falling steadily into the shadow of the step above. The same ramp serves any pitch.
- `cornerShade`: where a step meets the kerb (or the well's wall) there is a wedge of shadow widening toward the next step, so both edges of the flight are toothed. The test suite caught the first version of this putting shadow on the lip of the head's shallowest steps.
- `treadTone(level)`: every step is a shade lighter than the one below, counted from the Vestibule's floor, so the foot, the flight and the head are one ramp.
- `flightPix(fx, fy, W, first)`: one pixel of a flight `W` wide: kerbs along both sides that do not step, the wear pale and round-lipped down the middle of the *flight*, a chipped lip, pits, and one stone to a step, a few of them cracked through. (The first attempt jointed the treads into slabs; a course of short stones is a wall, and it read as brick. Joints went.)

The three users: **the flight** is the `FS.STAIR` tile. `tiles.ts: stairPix` finds the run of stair tiles the tile stands in (west, east and north until wall; memoised per tile like the rampart) and asks `flightPix` with the run's width and the step number, starting at `FOOT_STEPS`. A fall of rubble on a step does not end the run; nothing it reads changes when the stair is opened, so re-bakes stay local as before. **The foot** (`stairfoot`) is `flightPix` for a 36 px flight from level 0. **The head** (the open `buriedgate`) is the same step with a receding pitch (8, 7, 6, 5, 5, 4, 3, 3 rows), each step narrower and darker than the one above it, between the well's two walls (the east one lit, the west one shaded and shading the tread ends), down into the dark under the lintel.

### The fall, from above

Dig stage 3 is the heap that is met from below: `stoneBlob` boulders packed to the kerb over earth, a block or two of the vault's dressed stone and roots among them, exactly the makings of `rockpile`. Its layout comes from a fixed seed of its own, not the sprite's (which differs per stage), so stage 4 is the same heap with a breach in it rather than a different heap: the stones within the breach are left out and the open well, dimmed, shows through with split stone lying on its steps. Stages 0 to 2 are as they were.

### Goldens and tests

Two hashes moved, each checked against the tree as it was. Sprite by sprite (trace hash per kind, six variants, both states): of 141 kinds exactly six differ, `rock`, `ironore`, `boulder`, `outcrop`, `stairfoot` and `buriedgate`; none was added or removed. Pixel by pixel (both bakes on the pixel canvas, per tile): in the dungeon 24 tiles differ, the stair's own (34-37,50-55); on the surface none (of 6912), so its golden did not move. The legacy prop golden did not move either.

`tests/rockArt.test.ts` holds the rest: the new sprites' own goldens (the stair head's stages 4 and 5 included, which the registry golden does not reach); that it is art only (sizes, anchors, boxes, what is harvestable, every placed variant has a shape, twelve veins, `SAVE_VERSION`); on rasterised sprites, that a rock uses every tone, is lighter above than below, stands inside its sprite within a closed outline and emits nothing; that no two variants of a kind share a silhouette and a form is a function of its seed; that a vein's ore is a tenth to a quarter of it, shaded with the faces it crosses, its rust only ever below ore, and that no other rock has any; the step's ramp at every pitch; the flight's kerbs, toothed corners, wear, lightening and at most one crack a tread, never over the one below; that the foot's pixels and a baked run's pixels *are* `flightPix` (a fixture run in a wall, with rubble on one row), which is what "consistent" means here; and that the head darkens and narrows as it goes down, the glyph wakes only when open, and the fall is heaped, not coursed (no row of it is mostly joint; every seventh row of the old one was all joint).
